    How to read this page#

    • Entries are grouped by theme, then by article. Click the article title for the full text and its Sources section.
    • Core system articles (damage, status effects, items, talents and so on) show every entry. Articles about one enemy, boss, quest, NPC, lore topic or region show their first four entries and point to the rest.
    • For a quick start, read Damage formula, Poise and stagger, Status effects, Trophy reforging and New Game Plus first: these hold the mechanics that change builds most.

    Combat and damage#

    How hits, resistances, staggers and damage-over-time really work.

    Combat#

    • Every hit drains your sanity a little. In story mode (not in the Dark Forest), landing damage on an enemy costs you sanity equal to SanDamage × damage / enemy max HP, where SanDamage is set per enemy placement (4 for the first enemies).
    • Blocked and parried hits do no poise damage from the front, but they still deal their full weakness damage, so blocking a long string wears down the weakness gauge.
    • Your companions are untouchable in the story. Outside the maze, team members other than your own hero lose no health or sanity to any hit.
    • Long-range hits lose their side effects too. Moves with a distance falloff (such as Viola's shots, 3 to 15 m) scale rage gain, power gain, poise, weakness and sanity damage by the same falloff factor.
    • Rage on low health is 30%, not 50%. Trophies such as Raw Gem Amulet, Brass Mask and Flamel's Ring (logic type 125, +40%, or +55% on their upgraded versions) raise rage gain when your health is at or below 30%.
    • Being hit only fills your Rage with a talent. The damage code gives the target RageAddHit rage only if the target has a "rage when hit" talent such as Terrence's "Revenge" (+50% of RageAddHit per level).
    • "Loneliness Whisper" halves, it does not roll. Its description talks about a reduced chance of losing sanity. The code simply halves the sanity damage (SanSub) of every hit you take while you carry it.
    • Combo hits. From the 4th consecutive hit on the same target (combo counter above 3), talents of the "combo weakness" type add extra weakness damage. See Weakness and execution.
    • Boss time challenge "perfect" runs. In the boss time-challenge mode, any health damage to you (from enemies or traps) marks the run as not perfect.

    Critical hits#

    • The real chance is (rate + 1) / 1001. The game rolls a whole number from 0 to 1000 inclusive and crits when the roll is ≤ the rate. 0‰ never crits and 1000‰ or more always crits.
    • Crits come in one-second streaks (inferred from the code). Before every roll, the random generator is re-seeded from the current clock time in whole seconds plus the attacker's id.
    • Armour still reduces the poise-break crit. A guaranteed poise-break crit still goes through the target's move damage reduction (DamageSub, which stays active until the armour window closes), so armoured bosses such as Treasure Thieves (−90% during some moves) take only a fraction of even that crit.
    • "Bloody" is weaker than it reads. Its text says crit rate is increased by 20%, but the code multiplies your crit rate by 1.2 instead of adding 20 points: Viola's 64‰ becomes 76.8‰ (about 7.8%), not 26%.
    • Jerold's finishers can crit. The DLC hero's backstab, execution and plunging attacks lack the no-crit flag every other hero has on them, so they roll for crits like normal attacks.
    • Two crit talent types are never used. The code supports per-move "crit rate +" and "crit damage +" talents (SkillCritRateAdd, SkillCritDamageAdd), but no talent in the shipped talent table uses them.
    • "Crit resistance" works backwards. The stat is a multiplier on the attacker's crit bonus. 0 means no protection and lower non-zero values protect more. No hero or trophy has it, and its menu label was never translated.
    • Enemies almost never crit at random. About 2,680 of the 2,747 story enemy placements have a crit rate of 0‰ (2,607) or 1‰ (74; a 0.2% real chance), even though their crit damage is 212-250‰.

    Damage formula#

    • Defence can never cut damage below 30% of the attack value. If the attack is weaker than the target's defence, every hit deals a flat 30%, however much more defence the target stacks.
    • Flat move power matters early, multipliers late. Each move adds its own flat Attack (61 for Terrence's first slash) on top of the stat-based part, so at low levels most of your damage comes from the move itself.
    • Back hits are not only backstabs. Any normal attack that lands from behind gets ×1.25 (or ×2 on some moves) and goes straight through a guard. A few moves (BackGuardBreak 0) deal nothing from behind.
    • Hitting several weak points with one swing counts each one. The game runs the full damage calculation once for every weak-point hitbox the attack touches, each at ×2.
    • "Damage to Humans/Beasts/Sendrils" only boosts the stat part of a hit. The race bonus multiplies attack stat × BaseRate before the move's flat Attack is added.
    • Conditional trophy bonuses add up. Equipped trophies that share a logic type add their values before the bonus is applied: Bloodthirsty Eye + Beastheart Stone + Glowing Crystal give +37.5% attack in an abnormal sanity state.
    • Lunatic states change your stats, and so your damage. While in an abnormal sanity state, Terrence gains +10% of his base Physical attack and loses 30% of his max HP, Viola gains +10% of her base crit damage, Norwood loses 50% of his Fire/Poison/Arcane/Dark resistance and Jerold loses 50% of his Dark resistance.
    • Each weak point repeats the whole hit. Because the full calculation runs once per weak-point hitbox, a swing across two weak points also gives you Rage and Power twice, drains your sanity twice and deals poise and weakness damage twice.
    • Race bonuses use the absolute value. The helper that applies "+X‰" bonuses (MathHelper.addValuePermil) takes the absolute value of X, so a negative bonus would still increase damage. No shipped item has a negative value.
    • "Night's Embrace" turns targets into Sendrils. While a target has one of Jerold's Griminess statuses (10101/10102) and is not already a Sendril, this trophy treats it as a Sendril for your damage-vs-race bonuses: a Human or Beast becomes a Sendril only, but a target that is both Human and Beast keeps its Human flag and gains the Sendril flag.
    • Benita's shield burst. One of Benita's moves (SkillSP2, second hit) removes all of her shields and adds their total as flat damage to that hit, after every multiplier.
    • Some moves ignore defence entirely. A move whose DefenceType is 0 ("Other") makes the target's defence 0, so the factor is 1.
    • Dealing damage drains your sanity. In story mode (outside the Dark Forest), each hit you land costs you SanDamage × damage dealt / enemy max HP sanity, where SanDamage is set per enemy placement (4 for the first enemies).
    • Lifesteal is computed on the final pre-rounding damage. Benita's draining moves (SuckHp 1000 to 1500‰, i.e. 100% to 150% of damage dealt), the trophies Father's Love, Glory and Basilisk's Epiphany (+5% to +10%), and the "Discipline"-type trophies that instead cost you 5% to 12% of ...
    • Sanity-collapse doubles are glass cannons (inferred). Hostile hero copies created with the "SanMonster" monster type get their max HP halved and their Physical attack doubled (SanMonsterHp 500, SanMonsterPhysicDamage 2000, applied as value × ‰ / 1000).

    Damage types and resistances#

    • "Element Res" is really Fire resistance. The stat the menus call "Element Res" (and "Element Damage" for attack), named "Elemental resistance" (元素抗性) on trophy properties, is property 23.
    • Attack type and defence type are independent. A move can use one stat for attack and another resistance for defence.
    • Pierce is the enemies' hidden armour. Enemy Pierce resistance is many times their other defences (up to 20×).
    • No trophy can give you an elemental or Pierce attack. Random trophy properties only roll Attack, crit, HP, defence, the four resistances and the race bonuses (types 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 11, 15, 23-26, 35-37), never properties 19-22 or 31.
    • Resistance is read from the current maximum, rounded up. The defence value includes buffs, debuffs and lunatic-state changes, is rounded up and can't go below 0. When you are hit, the conditional trophies then multiply it.
    • Status damage ignores resistances. Bleed, Poison, Burn and other damage-over-time effects deal fixed damage and never go through this calculation. See Status effects.

    Backstab#

    • Rear hits deal +25 % damage. Any attack whose attacker stands behind the target's facing plane counts as "from behind".
    • This works both ways: enemies hitting you in the back also deal 1.25x.
    • Blocks do not work from behind. A hit from behind ignores your block or guard hand completely: no damage reduction, and Poise damage is not cancelled. See Guarding.
    • The same rule makes plunge attacks strong. Every hero's plunge attack (Drop, move 111) has BackGuardBreak = 2, so a plunge that lands behind the target doubles.
    • Norwood's oddity. His Backstab row uses 1.25 instead of 2. His backstab does only about 63 % of what an identical move would do with the x2 multiplier. Whether this is intended is unknown.
    • Zero multipliers are harmless in practice. 356 SkillData rows have BackGuardBreak = 0, so a rear hit from one of them would be multiplied by 0.
    • How enemies notice you (and so whether a Backstab prompt appears). An enemy acquires a target when you are inside its hearing range or its sight cone.

    Parry and counter#

    • Priority beats timing. A perfect counter window is useless against an enemy move with equal or higher value. Terrence and Viola (value 2) can answer only the 376 basic enemy attacks.
    • The counter cancels the damage completely. The hit is skipped before damage, status and Sanity are applied.
    • Parry needs facing. Both fighters must be facing within 90 degrees of opposite. Flanking attackers cannot be parried.
    • The 35 rule. A parry staggers into Weakened only when the enemy's Resilience is at or below 35.
    • Reflecting beats parrying. The projectile check ignores the middle-third window. Benita and Elena can swat shots all through their box time.
    • Nine enemy types cannot be parried at all (listed above).
    • Priority 3 and 5 enemies include the Holy Father (31 value-3 and 6 value-5 counterable rows), Jenny of Exilium (20 and 13), Fused Sprenger (22 and 7), the Usurper (20 and 11), Terror Obsession (18 and 10), Proxy Higgins (7 and 11) and The First Fallen: Handel (69 and 1).
    • A counter gives no Sanity. Every counter move has SanAdd 0.

    Poise and stagger#

    • Weak points. A few bosses have body colliders flagged as weak points.
    • The colliders themselves are part of the Unity models. Every other model has the default multiplier of 1 (x2), but no weak-point colliders as far as the data shows (inferred).
    • Blocking stops Poise damage only from the front. A blocked or hand-guarded hit from in front deals 0 Poise damage (it still drains Resilience, see Weakness and execution). A hit from behind ignores the block entirely.
    • Distance falloff. Moves with DamageDisMin/DamageDisMax scale Poise damage, Resilience damage, Rage and sanity damage by sqrt(1 - x^2), where x is the position between the two distances.
    • Moves that ignore Poise. 82 moves have ToughnessIgnore = 1 and always cause their hit reaction: every hero's Counter Attack, Backstab, Execution and plunge attack, plus a few enemy moves.
    • Moves that set their own Poise. A handful of enemy moves reset the user's Poise to a fixed value when they start (ToughnessSet).
    • Player Poise on Casual. Casual difficulty adds +130 Poise to the player character (+230 on a cloud demo build). Terrence's Poise Regen is 0, while every other hero has 240 (24 % of the pool per second).
    • Status effects. Jerold's Griminess (status 10031) is a max-Poise reduction status (LogicType 102 = PropertyMaxSub, Param1 = 13) that lasts 10 s.
    • Talents. SkillToughnessAdd adds armour to specific moves (Terrence's Knight's Tenacity on his heavy combos (value 15, ValueAdd 15; the level scaling is inferred), Rising Slash: Tenacity (value 38, ValueAdd 38) and Norwood/Jerold/Elena's Health Potion: Patience +100).
    • Hero doppelgangers. Copies of a player hero that the story controller spawns as "sanity monsters" get -14 Poise (SanMonsterHeroToughnessSub), so every hit staggers them.

    Weakness and execution#

    • Parry into Execution. If a parry box (Benita, Elena) catches an enemy attack while that enemy's Resilience is at or below 35 (ParryWeaknessMin), its Resilience is set to 0 and it reels into a weakened recoil, then into the Weakened state.
    • Counters into Weakened. If a successful Counter Attack hits an enemy whose Resilience is already 0, its recoil becomes the "hand weak" version, which leads into Weakened.
    • Norwood's Berserker Instinct. While Norwood is in the Abnormal state, the talent gives a chance (talent value 300; read as 30 %, inferred) that a hit sets the target's Resilience straight to 0.
    • Boss time-challenge fights (ES_StoryChallengeMonsterProperty, used when the story mode is BossTimeChallenge; whether this is the Obsession Challenge is inferred) add Resilience by challenge level: level 2 +25, level 3 +50 and level 4 +75, nothing at levels 1, 5 and 6.
    • Stamina keeps regenerating during an Execution. Attacks normally pause stamina regen, but potion drinking, Execute and Backstab are exempt from that rule (unless the post-action regen delay is still running).

    Difficulty#

    • Casual and the Egg reduce damage after every other calculation (armour, resistances, guarding), as the very last multipliers.
    • Casual does not touch enemy health, damage or AI. The whole difference is on your side.
    • The settings menu can only lower the difficulty. A Casual save can never earn the 31 Normal-only achievements, even after switching.
    • In Arcade, choosing Casual loads the Normal enemy table; Casual there only changes the player-side bonuses and the ledge-fall rule.

    Dodging#

    • Jerold's dodge only works if you time it. His basic dodge has no i-frames. Its protection comes from the perfect-dodge follow-up, so a late dodge with Jerold is just a quick step.
    • Viola's unused longer roll. Viola's dodges have a second, longer i-frame track (skillMuteki2: 0%–60% instead of 0%–46%, and 0%–41% instead of 0%–31%) that turns on with a "SkillMuteki" talent.
    • Norwood's i-frames start late. His dodge is vulnerable for its first 7.5%, so very late dodges can still be hit.
    • Dodge cost is fixed at 65 (Elena 48) and does not scale with equipment weight. Trophies reduce it: Eternity and Concentration Rune −15%, Broken Eternity −9%, Chaos Silk −20%.
    • Jerold's dodge chain counter. Jerold counts consecutive dodges. After 3 in a row, the game changes his dodge's skill condition until he has gone 1 second without dodging (DodgeResetCntMax = 3, DodgeResetCntTime = 1).
    • A dodge needs only 1 stamina. Like every action, a dodge can start with any stamina above 0.

    Guarding#

    • Poise is safe, Weakness is not. A successful block or deflect deals 0 poise damage, but the attack's Weakness damage still applies.
    • Guard Break numbers. Across all enemy attack rows, the most common Guard Break is 50 (exactly Terrence's guard, so no chip). About two-thirds of enemy attacks exceed 50 and cause chip damage on Terrence.
    • Attacks from behind ignore guards. They are never blocked and deal BackGuardBreak × BackDamageRate times normal damage. Most player attacks have BackGuardBreak 1.25, and backstab-type moves have 2. See Backstab.
    • Norwood's and Elena's failed guards. A failed guard (diff ≥ 2.5) scales damage by GuardDamageRatio, not GuardBreakDamageRatio.
    • Norwood's guard break in the lunatic state. When Norwood is lunatic, a broken guard also sets his Weakness bar to 0.
    • Guard regeneration is slow. While the guard is up, stamina regenerates at 10%/s instead of 40%/s.
    • Talents that change guarding: Terrence's Parry: Enhanced (talent 11203, its text says 30% less stamina consumed on a successful parry; the code applies it as a 300 per-mille cut to the stamina lost on the affected guard move).
    • Parry is part of the guard. Terrence's guard raise has a counter window from 11.7% to 25.5% of the animation, and Elena's guard has a parry window. Timing a hit there triggers a parry instead of a block.

    Rage#

    • The 99 cap before the tutorial. In story mode, Rage is held at max − 1 (99) until the hero's Rage tutorial quest is completed. Until then, moves that need a full 100 cannot be used.
    • Getting hit gives no Rage by default. Taking damage only grants Rage with a talent such as Terrence's Revenge. That gives enemy move's RageAddHit × 500 / 1000.
    • Long-range falloff. For moves with a damage range (DamageDisMin/DamageDisMax), hitting from beyond the maximum range grants a flat 1 Rage.
    • Low-health bonus. A trophy effect (logic type 125, "HP half Rage add") raises Rage gain by 40%, 55% or 70% depending on the trophy tier (Raw Gem Amulet, Brass Mask and Flamel's Ring carry it).
    • Flat talent bonuses add to each hit: Terrence Furor +2, Benita Rhythm: Pure Blood +4, Benita Forced Bloodsucking +5 on backstab and execution, Norwood Craze +15 on backstab and execution, Jerold Craze +5.
    • Persistence. Your Rage is saved with the hero and kept through rests, loading and switching areas. Respawning after death resets it to 0.
    • Rage thresholds. Some talents raise the Weakness damage of a move when Rage is above 33% or above 66% of max. Elena's attack audio also changes at the same 33% and 66% marks.
    • Elena's Rage tiers. Several steps of Elena's double-blade five-hit chains have a Required Rage of 33 or 66 and cost 0, so the chain gets longer as the gauge fills.
    • Lunatic Jerold loses 10 Rage when he is hit during a move that has armor (the animator's skillToughness flag; inferred wording). See Lunatic state.

    Stamina#

    • Overspending is free. The game only checks that stamina is above 0. Starting a 180-cost charged attack with 5 stamina left is allowed; you just end at 0.
    • Exhaustion delay. When the bar hits exactly 0, regeneration is locked for clamp(100 − EnergyRecoveryTime, 0, 100) / 10 seconds.
    • The real regen pause is the animation. Regeneration stays off until the current move's run-exit frame. Chaining attacks keeps the bar frozen; stepping out between swings lets it refill.
    • Guarding at 0 stamina does nothing. A block or a hit-deflect only counts if stamina is above 0.
    • Stamina from critical hits. The trophies Oath (+15) and The First Seed (+35) restore that much stamina on each critical hit you land with a weapon (not with throwables).
    • Cheaper dodges. Eternity and Concentration Rune (−15%), Broken Eternity (−9%) and Chaos Silk (−20%) reduce the stamina cost of dodges only (dodge, dodge-cancel, and Jerold's leaping dodges). See Trophy effects.
    • Free actions. A status of type EnergyDontUse makes every action cost 0 stamina while it lasts. Benita's statuses 10022–10024 and Elena's status 10114 are of this kind.
    • Jerold's combo discount. A Jerold talent reduces stamina costs by a per-mille amount once he has chained 3 or more attacks (TalentComboAttackNum = 3).
    • Wheel of Reversal. For Jerold, this trophy restores a flat 50 stamina every time he triggers a perfect ("extreme") dodge. See Dodging.

    Bleed#

    • Bloody ignores half the bleeds. It checks a hard-coded list (BloodIdList) that does not include the Huge Fang bleed (10071), the side-effect bleeds or Handel's bleed. A Huge Fang bleed does not raise Viola's crit rate.
    • Two different bleeds cancel each other. An aimed shot (10001) on an enemy bleeding from red bullets (10034) ends that bleed and starts a fresh 8 s one, and the reverse is also true.
    • The side-effect bleed cannot kill you. Rows 10086/10087 have NotDeath, so the ticks stop at 1 HP.
    • Your own talents make the side effect worse. Elixir of Epiphany (20024, 21024) is on the item lists of "Prolonged Duration" and "Improved Effect".
    • Dodge the side effect entirely. The self-bleed (10086/10087) is flagged as a debuff but the Epiphany attack buff (10064/10065) is not.
    • Handel's frenzy bleed (10107) starts when he enters his rampage phase together with Dying Frenzy (10108), and drains 0.3% of his HP per second until the fight ends.
    • Bleed ignores physical buffs and debuffs. Its damage uses base physical resistance, so Corrosion on the target does not change bleed ticks.

    Burn#

    • Fire burns are resisted by physical resistance. Rows 10008, 10037 and 10078 have AttackType 2 (fire) but DefenceType 1 (physical).
    • Norwood's Abnormal-state resistance penalty does not apply to enemy burns, because it only affects non-physical defence types (see Norwood).
    • Fireworks cancel burns. The Fireworks Barrel's status is in the same family: throwing one at a burning enemy ends the burn and replaces it with the stagger drain.
    • Boss fire burns scale with the New Game+ cycle, not with the boss. Proxy Higgins' and Fused Sprenger's burns use fixed per-cycle trick damage (see above), so their tick depends on the cycle, not on the boss's stats.
    • On Casual player burn ticks are × 0.65, and × 0.5 with the Egg of the Bearer (see Difficulty).

    Corrosion#

    • Corrosion affects hits, not damage over time. Direct hits use your current physical defence, so Corrosion makes them hurt more. Bleed and Burn ticks use base physical defence, so Corrosion does not raise them.
    • Enemy Corrosion replaces Brute Force's penalty. Both are family 103. An enemy Corrosion hit while the Brute Force side effect is active deletes the side effect (defence back up) and starts a 25% Corrosion gauge.
    • Talents make Brute Force's penalty last longer. Brute Force (20014, 21014) is on the item list of "Prolonged Duration" (ItemStatusTimeAdd) (+10% per level: up to +30% for Terrence, Viola and Benita, +20% for Norwood and Jerold, +15% for Elena; "Improved Effect" reaches +30% for every hero).
    • Dodge the penalty entirely. The defence-down side effects (10084/10085) are flagged as debuffs, but the Brute Force attack buffs (10057/10058) are not.
    • The Treasure Thieves weaken themselves. When the Black Sprite King enters its weak condition it applies −40% physical defence (10110) and a Weakness cut (10111) to itself. Both are removed when it changes condition.
    • The Brute Force penalty is shown with the Corrosion icon (10003) but without the corrosion visual effect.

    Poison#

    • Antitoxin does not protect against the ticks. The tick formula reads base poison resistance, and potion resistance is a temporary change on top of it.
    • The pool's own poison roots you. Status 10076 is the game's StatusIdSlow: once its gauge is full and it becomes active, you are limited to walking and dodging is restricted, just like on slow mud.
    • Norwood in the Abnormal state (sanity 70% or lower, Lunatic included) has his poison resistance cut by 50% for the tick formula, or by 30% / 10% with "Adapt to Abnormality: Inertia" (the talent text says 40% / 20%).
    • On Casual player poison ticks are × 0.65, and × 0.5 with the Egg of the Bearer (see Difficulty).
    • The first hit decides the damage. The enemy's poison damage is captured on the first build-up hit; refreshing never recalculates it.
    • Where A comes from. Enemy moves and the traps they place use the enemy's poison damage. Tricks and the acid rain (a trap made by trick 10006, with no owner) use a fixed per-cycle damage instead: 100/125/150/175 for the rain.
    • Bomb-man poison explodes in New Game+. Tricks 10052-10054 jump from 125 to 800 damage after the first cycle.
    • Different poisons override each other. An instant 10042 hit on someone already poisoned by 10002 ends that poison and starts a new 12 s one.

    Status effects#

    • Resistance buffs do not reduce DoT damage. DoT ticks read base resistance, so Antitoxin Potion (+55 poison resistance) and Elixir of Inertia (+55 fire resistance) only reduce the damage of the poison or fire hit, never the poison or burn ticks.
    • Corrosion does not make DoTs hurt more. It lowers current physical defence, and DoTs read base defence. It does make every direct hit hurt more.
    • Burn is mostly reduced by physical resistance, not fire resistance. Burns 10008, 10037 and 10078 deal fire damage but use DefenceType 1 (physical).
    • The damage is decided by the first hit. The attacker's damage is read when the gauge first appears. Buffs or debuffs the attacker gets later do not change it, even after many refreshes.
    • A different variant cancels a running one. For example Viola's aimed-shot bleed (10001) and her red-bullet bleed (10034) are different ids of the same family: landing one on an enemy that is actively bleeding from the other ends that bleed and restarts build-up.
    • Refreshes never stack damage. A bleeding enemy hit ten more times still takes one tick per second; the hits only push the end time back.
    • Item status talents also boost item side effects. The talents "Prolonged Duration" (ItemStatusTimeAdd) and "Improved Effect" (ItemStatusValueAdd) list Brute Force and Elixir of Epiphany among their items.
    • Norwood in the Abnormal state takes extra damage from non-physical DoTs: his resistance counts at 50% for the tick formula (Hero10004_Define.SanResistSubRate = 500).
    • Invincibility does not stop ticks. Dodge invincibility and "invincible" buffs do not block damage over time.
    • Shields soak ticks first. Each HP tick is taken from a skill shield, then from a trophy shield, before it reaches HP (see Shields and protection).
    • Some DoTs cannot kill. Rows with NotDeath (only the elixir side-effect bleeds 10086/10087) stop at 1 HP. Every other DoT can kill.
    • Debuff immunity first, elixir second. The side effects of Brute Force (10084/10085, defence down) and Elixir of Epiphany (10086/10087, self-bleed) are flagged as debuffs, but the attack buffs they come with (10057/10058, 10064/10065) are not.
    • Trick and trap DoTs grow with New Game+. Ownerless sources use the trick's or trap's Damage for the current cycle.
    • Obsession Challenge: in the boss time challenge, any damage-over-time tick that you did not cause yourself (enemy, trick or trap) costs you the "perfect" result, just like a hit.
    • Nothing ticks on the hidden or the dying. Statuses are not updated while their bearer is hidden or dying, so no ticks happen.
    • The hero sanity effects start at the Abnormal state, not at Lunatic. Every hero-specific check used by statuses (IsHeroSanNotNormal) is true whenever sanity is not normal, i.e. from 70% sanity down (see Abnormal state, Lunatic state).
    • Benita's drug tolerance (Abnormal state) weakens statuses flagged DrugFast. No HP-damaging status has that flag.

    Blind#

    • Blind is not flagged as a debuff (IsDebuffer 0), so Debuff Immunity does not stop it from being applied.
    • The AI reacts to the start of blindness only. It keeps an internal "was blind" flag, so re-throwing smoke on an enemy that is still blind only extends the timer. It does not trigger a second retreat.
    • A blinded enemy that is mid-attack still finishes that attack. The lose-target check waits until the enemy is not using a skill (inferred from the IsSkilling check on the same branch).

    Buffs#

    • One buff per family, and the newest wins. Statuses in the same family (StatusType) replace each other. The game never compares strength: it removes the old buff and starts the new one.
    • All four resistance elixirs are one family (301). An Antitoxin Potion removes your Fire Resistance, so you can only have one elemental resistance buff at a time.
    • Elixir of Stamina and Elixir of Energy are one family (303). Drinking one cancels the other.
    • Health regeneration (300) covers the Large Health Potion's regeneration too.
    • Different families add up. Buffs of the same stat from different families are summed. Brute Force (+35 attack) plus the maze Attack Up (+17.5% of base) plus Benita's or Viola's skill buffs all stack.
    • Re-using the same item resets the timer. The duration goes back to full. It does not add to the remaining time.
    • The item talents are bigger than they look, but miss some items. Every hero has Prolonged Duration (+10/20/30% duration, 27 items) and Improved Effect (+10/20/30% strength, 24 items).
    • Because of the round-up, rank 1 already doubles the Elixir of Sanity. Durations become 33/36/39 s (30 s items) and 66/72/78 s (60 s items).
    • Not covered by Improved Effect: the maze copies (72xxx), Large Health Potion's regeneration, Elixir of Energy, Potion of Wealth, Hound Amulet, Glowstone, Vertebra Nail, Slightly Sweet Roots and Shriveled Holy Fruit.
    • The talents also boost the drawbacks. Side effects are applied by the same item, so they get the same bonuses. Epiphany's self-bleed becomes 1.65/1.8/1.95% of base HP per second and lasts longer.
    • Avoiding the side effects. The defence and bleed side effects are flagged as debuffs.
    • Side effects share families with enemy debuffs. Epiphany's bleed is in the Bleed family (101) and Brute Force's defence loss is in the Corrosion family (103).
    • Benita in her Lunatic state: while she has Drug Tolerance, elixir effects flagged DrugFast (HP regeneration, resistances, attack, defence, stamina, sanity) work at half strength. See Benita.
    • Viola needs the talent. Viola gets no crit buff from a backstab or execution unless she has Break Out. Rank 1 gives 10038 and rank 2 gives 10039.
    • Some buffs survive scene changes and saves. The six statue buffs (10091-10096), the stacking death ward, and also the Hound Amulet (10099) and Glowstone (10098) buffs are flagged shared and saved.
    • Talent text that doesn't match the data. Elixir: Lingering Wind Whisper claims +33.3% / +66.6% duration. The data gives 40 s, then 50 s and 60 s, which is +25% / +50%.

    Sanity loss (status)#

    • Elixirs cancel each other. The Elixir of Hallucination (10074) shares family 306 with Elixir of Sanity (10009, +1/s for 15 s) and Vertebra Nail (10100, +5/s for 3 s). Using any of them replaces the others.
    • Debuff Immunity does not help against suction. The enemy Sanity Loss (10040) and the Hallucination drain are not flagged as debuffs. Slightly Sweet Roots neither removes nor blocks them.
    • Invincibility does not stop the ticks. Status104 contains a check that zeroes the tick for an invincible victim, but it sits in GetValueByPropertyId, which nothing in the game calls.
    • Talents make the Hallucination stronger. The Elixir of Hallucination (20021) is on the item lists of both item talents in every hero's tree. Improved Effect (+10/20/30%) raises the drain to 6/6/7 per second (rounded up).
    • Benita's drug tolerance halves it. While Benita is in her abnormal-sanity state and has a Drug Tolerance status (10044-10046), every item status flagged DrugFast, including Hallucination and the sanity elixirs, works at 50% strength.
    • Sanity does not drain in some places. All sanity loss, these statuses included, is ignored in Arcade mode (except boss fights), in dream stages, after the host has died, and while attacks are globally stopped.
    • The maze buffs only touch the maze's own drain. The Purifying Saint's "slower sanity loss" buff (10096, -50%) and the no-sanity-loss mark (10097) act on the Dark Mist maze's passive sanity drain.
    • Build-up for pools. Pools and other continuous sources add their build-up per second rather than per hit.

    Shields and protection#

    • Trophy shields and the trophy death ward refill only when you rest at an altar. Equipping the trophy does nothing until the next rest (the rest handler is the only code that applies these statuses; inferred that nothing else does).
    • Shields don't add together. Applying a shield status first sets its pool to 0, then fills it.
    • The big trophy shield blocks the others. Trophy Shield (500), Trophy Shield (small) and the Potion Shield are one family (112).
    • Eating a Shriveled Holy Fruit while the 500-point trophy shield is active does nothing, although the fruit is still used up (inferred: the item is consumed before its status is applied).
    • Wearing Double-star Brooch together with Basilisk's Protection or Halo Pendant is useless. At rest, the 500 shield is applied first and the 250 shield is rejected.
    • Eating the fruit while the 250-point brooch shield is active replaces it with a full 400-point Potion Shield.
    • Skill and trophy shields do stack. They are separate pools, so Benita's Holy Soul shield sits on top of a trophy or potion shield.
    • Benita's Annihilation talent spends both pools. Its damage equals the skill shield plus the trophy shield remaining. Using it empties both pools, which removes your trophy shield until the next altar rest.
    • Death wards don't save you from falls. The ward is skipped when the HP loss is fall damage. Hits that are meant to kill outright are tagged with a reason that the game never defines.
    • Debuff Immunity has blind spots. It blocks only statuses flagged as debuffs.
    • Invincibility doesn't stop statuses or DoT. Norwood's 1.5 s invincibility (status 10082, logic type 115 NoDamage) zeroes the damage of hits and blocks new hit reactions, but AddStatus never checks it (inferred), and the damage-over-time ticks of Bleed, Poison, sanity drain and ...
    • The maze death ward is generous. Each use of the Cleansing Saint statue adds a charge, up to 3, and the ward is saved and carried from floor to floor until used. When it saves you, it loses one charge.

    Stun#

    • Blocking does not stop stun build-up. When a hit lands, the game skips its status only if the hit was blocked or parried and the status row has GuardEnable set. Every stun has GuardEnable = 0.
    • No resistance. The gauge gain is the raw StackAddPer value. No stat, resistance or difficulty setting reduces it.
    • Getting hit breaks the stun. When a victim leaves the Freeze state for any other hit reaction (a flinch, knockback, launch and so on), the game clears every stun on it at once.
    • One stun at a time. All six stuns are in the same family (StatusType 109). A stun with a different ID replaces the current one.
    • A half-built gauge carries over. If a different stun in the same family lands while the current one is still only a gauge (not yet frozen), the old gauge is moved onto the new stun and the new hit's build-up is added to it (AddStatus keeps GetStack() of the replaced status).
    • Repeat stuns reset the timer. The same stun landing again while active resets its duration to full (IsRefresh 1).
    • The drain delay counts from the first hit. The short delay before the gauge starts draining (0.1 s for Frollo's stuns) is set when the gauge is created. Later hits do not restart it.
    • Debuff rules apply. Stuns are flagged as debuffs. While Slightly Sweet Roots' Debuff Immunity is active (10068, 30 s; 10123, 300 s for the + version), no stun can be applied, and eating the roots removes a current stun.
    • Invincibility does not cancel a stun. Norwood's 1.5 s counter invincibility blocks damage but does not touch status logic (inferred: the stun code never checks it).

    Abnormal state#

    • The penalty talents are reductions. The English text of "Adapt to Abnormality" can be read as stacking more penalty; in the code each one lowers the penalty by multiplying it by (1 - talent percent).
    • Wounds on Viola ignore blocking. The check that lets a block cancel the wound looks at a "guard" flag that this status does not have, so even a perfectly timed block still wounds her.
    • Benita's resistance starts working as soon as one stack exists. The text says healing is halved "finally", but the potion code halves the heal whenever any of the three resistance statuses is present (inferred: the status lists are not checked for a minimum stack).
    • Terrence's dodge restriction is data, not code. Two dodge rows carry a "normal only" flag; the talent adds a permission for those two moves.
    • "Lucky Madness" is not limited to this state. The drop code adds its bonus on every kill. See Sanity talents and trophies.
    • An unused Viola constant. Her define file also holds a 30% item-value reduction (SanItemValueSubRate 300) that no code reads; only Benita's 50% version is used.
    • Norwood's Toughness bonus is a flat point value. It is +10 points (+15 with the talent), not a percentage like every other penalty on this page.

    Lunatic state#

    • The floor is half the maximum, not zero. Refills stop at 50. Lunatic therefore feels like a timer, and the Lunatic doppelganger is the only cheap way to cancel it in the open world.
    • Hero swapping is disabled. While Lunatic you cannot rotate team members; this matters for builds that rely on a second hero.
    • The Lunatic slow trophy does not stack. Only one effect applies, so equipping two copies wastes a slot.
    • The lock is exactly half, never rounded. Refills stop at 0.5 x maximum (52.5 / 55 / 57.5 with the Sanity talent), while the Lunatic test rounds its bound up (53 / 55 / 58), so the lock can never lift you out.
    • The drain is a flat number, not a percentage. A 115-point maximum (three levels of the Sanity talent) gives a longer pool above 0 but the same 0.5 per second.
    • Boss phase two is player-controlled. Staying above the line keeps the first form; this is why avoiding sanity damage on bosses is a real strategy.
    • **Constant SanDeadRate (0.6) and the sanity-first-kill reduction table are unused.**

    Sanity doppelganger#

    • It is always your own hero. The branch that would spawn a story enemy instead is unreachable, because the index is always clamped to the number of heroes you own.
    • It never appears in boss rooms. There the Lunatic state is handled by promoting the boss to its second phase instead.
    • Several unlocked heroes randomise it. The copy can be any hero in your party, not necessarily the one you are playing.
    • It is a glass cannon. Half health, double damage and almost no stagger gauge.

    Sanity in special areas#

    • Maze refills double as a cure. Because the game adds the gap to 50, the cheapest maze sanity source (a single kill) is enough to end the Lunatic state.
    • Moon vs. Immortal jellyfish jars. The game has two separate jar items. The Moon Jellyfish Jar (maze only) blocks drain.
    • The Lost Valley drain ignores your sanity stage. Normal heroes lose sanity there at the same rate as Lunatic ones; the only safe places are the circles.
    • Colossal Grave Lunatic is free. You get Lunatic bonuses without the drain or the doppelganger.
    • One unidentified area. Story id 101, absent from the story table, has 16 enemy placements that give +3 sanity per kill, the only non-maze, non-boss area where kills restore sanity.

    Sanity talents and trophies#

    • Melos Bottles turn the Abnormal state on permanently. Because Abnormal trophies, hero bonuses and the enemy "crazy" flag all key off the state, a bottle plus four +20% attack trophies gives +80% attack for free; the cost is the hero's penalty and being one threshold from Lunatic state.
    • Lunatic-survival trophies are the only ones that do not stack. The equip code takes the maximum for trophies flagged "no overlay".
    • Devout is much smaller than it looks. It only affects the damage-to-sanity rule, which exists only in the main campaign.
    • Improved Effect and Prolonged Duration reach the sanity potions. Both talents list the Elixir of Sanity and Elixir of Hallucination (not the Vertebra Nail). Prolonged Duration lengthens them by 10% / 20% / 30% per level.
    • "Adapt to Abnormality" talents are penalty reducers, not extra penalties (see Abnormal state).

    Sanity#

    • Campaign kills never refill sanity. The "SanKill" gain is zero for every ordinary enemy in chapters 1 to 9, and the only non-zero rows there are the four chapter bosses (+200) and the two club-elite entries (+200).
    • The sanity cost is proportional to damage dealt. Weak enemies you barely scratch cost almost nothing; a huge-health enemy costs the same total as a small one.
    • Large damage-to-sanity numbers on bosses are safe. In a boss fight the minimum is pinned at the Lunatic bound, so a "130" boss cannot kill you through sanity.
    • Sanity potions ignore your defences. The sanity statuses (Elixir of Sanity, Elixir of Hallucination, Vertebra Nail, the ground effects) are tagged with the physical defence type, but their defence scale (Param3) is 0, and the status code multiplies your resistance by that scale before using it.
    • "Improved Effect" doubles the Elixir of Sanity. It multiplies the per-second value by 1.1 / 1.2 / 1.3 (talent levels 1 to 3) and the result is rounded up, so the 1 always becomes 2: the elixir gives about 30 instead of 15 over its duration.
    • Benita's potion resistance also halves sanity elixirs. While she is Abnormal or Lunatic and carries any of her drug-resistance stacks, every potion-type status tick is halved before rounding up: Hallucination 5 becomes 3, the Elixir of Sanity stays at 1 (even with Improved Effect, 1.1 x 0.5 rounds up to 1).
    • The Lunatic lock is not rounded. Refills stop at exactly 0.5 x maximum (57.5 with three Sanity talent levels), while the Lunatic test rounds its bound up (58), so the lock can never lift you out of the state.
    • Statue and frog numbers are on a doubled scale. The code halves every sanity cost and gain printed on these offers (inferred reason: they were written for a 0 to 200 scale), so a "Dedicate 10 Sanity" statue really takes 5.
    • Several talent descriptions point the wrong way. "Adapt to Abnormality" talents read as additional penalties but are reductions of the penalty.
    • Lost Valley and the maze never charge for damage. Sanity-by-damage is disabled in the Lost Valley, and maze monsters carry a SanDamage of 0.
    • Unused data. The constants for a "sanity first-kill" reduction table (10 / 5 / 1 kills, 100% / 60% / 20%), SanDeadRate 0.6, and the Terrence dodge-close rate 700 are defined but never read.

    Characters, stats and talents#

    Heroes, attributes, leveling and the talent trees.

    Characters#

    • Fixed pools. Every hero has 100 maximum Rage, Sanity 100 and Power 100 as fixed values that do not come from the six attributes. Elena's Rage bar marks its threshold at 33 instead of 30 (UIRageMin).
    • Only Terrence has 3 potions. The extra slot is plain data: HpAddNum is 3 for Terrence and 2 for the rest.
    • Elena is built differently. Her starting attributes add up to 110 against about 70 for everyone else, with Vigor 34 and Endurance 24, so she starts with 1,392 Health and 104 Attack.
    • Guard stamina ratios. The stamina-cost ratio for blocking (GuardEnergyRatio) is 0.35 for Terrence, Viola and Benita, 0.45 for Norwood and 0.25 for Jerold and Elena. See Guarding.
    • Only Terrence never regains Poise. Poise Regen is 0 for Terrence and 240 (24% of the pool per second) for the other five; Poise itself starts at 0 for all of them. See Poise and stagger.
    • Hero id gates. Available is read by MS_HeroData.IsAvailableInScene as a "minimum story id" test, which is why Norwood (3) and Jerold (9) behave as later unlocks (inferred from the function name and values).

    Terrence#

    • Three free Health Potion slots. Terrence starts with a capacity of 3 Health Potions, the other heroes with 2; every hero has MpAddNum 4, a separate second potion pool whose exact in-game item I did not trace (unverified).
    • Flat power rules the early game. At the starting Attack of 59, a Light hit's raw attack is 61 + 59 x 0.8 = 108: more than half is the flat part.
    • Rage per stamina favours Heavy. A Light hit gives 3 Rage for 65 stamina (0.046 per stamina); a Heavy hit gives 6 Rage for 95 (0.063), about 37% better. A full Heavy chain is 18 Rage, a full Light chain 9.
    • The Lunge is the most stamina-efficient art. At 59 Attack it deals about 2.65 raw attack per stamina (252 for 95), against 2.35 for Discipline, 1.98 for the full Rising Slash and 1.57 for the short one.
    • Armour mostly grows with Rage cost. Light attacks have no armour at all, the Heavy chain 26, the 30-Rage arts Lunge and Discipline 64 and the 100-Rage arts (Multi Lunge, Sanction) 94.
    • Rear hits. Every move row has a rear multiplier (BackGuardBreak). Most moves deal x1.25 when they land on an enemy's back and Backstab and the Jump chop deal x2; thrown items have x1.
    • Counter finisher is tied to its target. The second part of the Counter Attack (power 181) can only hit enemies that were just struck by the first part (power 136); other enemies in the way are ignored by it (Hero10001:IsMutekiForSkill).
    • Moves that cannot crit or be out-poised. Counter Attack, Backstab, Execution and the Jump chop have CritIgnore 1 and ToughnessIgnore 1: they never crit, and they stagger the target whatever its Poise.
    • Dodge chains fall back. If Spin Attack is not unlocked, Dodge then Heavy simply starts the ordinary Heavy combo. If it is unlocked, Spin Attack (priority 10) wins.
    • No second Rage payment after Rising Slash. The data row that opens the Heavy combo after Rising Slash carries a 30-Rage requirement, but the chain table sends Rising Slash (and its short and Enhanced versions) straight into the second row of that combo, which has no requirement.
    • "Not enough Rage" warning only for three moves. Only Lunge, Discipline and Lunge: Knockback have the Tips flag that shows the Rage-insufficient message; the other arts just fail silently.
    • Poise never regenerates for him. Terrence's base Poise is 0 and his Poise Regen is 0. On Casual difficulty he receives +130 Poise that also never regenerates (see Poise and stagger and Difficulty).
    • Stamina recovery. Regeneration is 400 per mille of the bar per second (121 per second at 303 Stamina), but only 100 per mille while guarding.
    • Breakables. Every attack row also has a DurabilitySub (40 to 999), which the object code (shell.lua) subtracts from destructible objects' durability.
    • Start unlocks. Lunge, Parry and Execution are the only combat moves that begin unlocked. Everything else, including Backstab and Counter Attack, costs talent nodes.

    Viola#

    • Rage engine. Her Light chain gives 12 Rage per hit (Terrence 3), the Counter Attack, Backstab and Execution 10 each and the Jump chop 5. Bullets give none.
    • Arts and counters do not use ammo. The Light Combo shot, Powerful Shooting shot and Counter Attack shot are fired by the code without taking a round (ammo is subtracted only for the Heavy shots and the Dodge shot), yet they use the selected colour's damage row.
    • Snipe costs Rage, not a round. The aimed shot spends 100 Rage and 80 stamina but takes no round from the magazine; it only needs at least one round loaded to start (or the talent's reload-then-aim variants).
    • Light Combo's shot is conditional. The bayonet hit goes into the follow-up shot only if the target's Poise is empty after the hit (the normal version deals 30 Poise damage; the white Weakening version only 15).
    • Rapid-Fire loads min(6, rounds in magazine). One round per animation trigger while Special is held, with a firing phase that spends one round per trigger.
    • Counter window comes from the reload. Her Counter Attack triggers from moves whose counter level is higher than the enemy's: the Special-button reload (move 1) and the Rapid-Fire preparation (moves 6 to 11) have level 2, while normal attacks have 1.
    • Lucky Shooter is only for Heavy shots. It is rolled in the function that subtracts a single round (Heavy shots and Dodge shot), with a (200 + 1) / 1001 chance; Rapid-Fire uses a different function with no roll.
    • Ammo drop maths. A kill gives add = 1 + the Bullets Recycling value (0 / 1 / 2), the chance is add x 100 per mille, so 10.1% / 20.1% / 30.1% (a number from 0 to 1000 is rolled and must be at most the chance), and the drop contains add rounds.
    • Hold-Heavy starts belong to the Snipe talent. The hold-Heavy entries (move 3 row 1, moves 29, 30 and 31) all need 100 Rage and carry the talent flag, and the Snipe talent opens exactly moves 2, 3, 29, 30 and 31.
    • Coloured Knockback range. In the data, the red Powerful Shooting: Knockback shot has a falloff range of 4 to 6 m, far smaller than every other red shot (4 to 20 m). It looks like a data quirk (inferred).
    • Poise is not part of her kit. Her base Poise is 0 and her Poise Regen is 240 per mille, so regeneration does nothing (240 per mille of 0). See Poise and stagger.
    • Unused healing penalty. A constant for 30% less healing sits in her define file (SanItemValueSubRate), but the lunatic text never mentions it and only Benita's constants of that kind are used in the item and status code (a search for the Viola constant finds no use).
    • Health Potions. She has capacity 2 (HpAddNum), like every hero except Terrence (3). MpAddNum is 4 for every hero; I did not trace what that second pool is (unverified).

    Benita#

    • The Health tax is a percentage of maximum Health, charged per swing. Light costs 2%, Heavy and the Whip of Monstrance family 3%, the Counter start 10%. It never kills her (minimum 1 Health).
    • Lifesteal numbers are large. Touch of Whip heals 100% of the damage dealt (150% on its rhythm version). Bloodsucking adds 80% / 100% / 120% to everything for 6 / 9 / 12 s.
    • Intellect is the attack stat. An Intellect point is worth many times a Strength point for her (rate 1000 against 200), and Intellect also raises Crit damage and all resistances.
    • Shield strength scales with Vigor. The Holy Soul shield is a percentage of her base Skill shield value; trophies that raise Skill shield may raise it too (inferred: the shield reads the base value of the Skill shield property).
    • Wind Prayer says it needs Wind Whisper. The talent text tells you to hold the button while Wind Whisper is active; the skill data only requires Elixir mode 1 and 30 charge (inferred reading: "Wind Whisper" is the mode name).
    • Wind Prayer's upgrade tiers do not exist. Three Perpetual statuses (30 / 40 / 50 s) are in the data but the code and talents only reach the first, because no talent changes the status the move applies.
    • Elixirs need only 1 charge. The cost is paid down to zero. Only Wind Prayer actually needs its full 30.
    • No Rage drain, no Power drain. Pure Blood and Elixir charge stay until spent. Her Rage bar only fills from hits and from Backstab or Execution. The decay routines run only for Norwood, Jerold and Elena.
    • A trophy can force the lunatic state. The Sanity check returns the "Unusual" band for the player's hero whenever a trophy with the always-abnormal logic is equipped (outside the Carriage), whatever the real Sanity is.
    • Lunatic penalties are host-only. The abnormal-state code checks that Benita is the hero the player controls.
    • AI Benita uses Elixirs only when none is running. The combat AI checks for her Bloodsucking, Rejuvenation and Perpetual statuses before using the Stamina elixirs, and for any Shield before using the shield.
    • Elixir pulses hit. Every Elixir move deals a small 61 + 80% Attack hit around her and gives Pure Blood (10 for the shield).

    Norwood#

    • Heavy attacks cost Griminess to even start. The first row of every Heavy swing requires 20 Griminess and the release spends 25. The Heavy triple needs this for each of its three swings.
    • Perfect guards refill him. A block whose block parameter is above 1 (the animation's tight timing window, which I take to be the perfect guard; inferred) gives +12 Griminess and 30% of max Stamina (the stamina ratio BlockAddEnergy 300 per mille), so guarding well is a Griminess source as well as a defence.
    • Liberate is a timer, and the timer is Rage. Entering needs a full 100 Rage.
    • The Rage guard falls back to the normal guard. The Liberate guard has strength 1000, far above any attack, but each hit it blocks costs 10 Rage; at 0 Rage the code swaps it for the ordinary guard (75).
    • Revenge is a counter and a finisher. Its first row has armour 999, Guard Break 999 and an interrupt value of 10, with 50% damage reduction while armoured (DamageSub 500; every Liberate punch has the same 50%, as do the two potion-drinking rows).
    • Intercepting rewrites the move. When a Coffin swing (any of the four families, the dodge swing or Poised Furious Griminess) is hit during its first part at the right time, the game plays a short freeze, jumps straight to the recharge-and-release combos, plays the "stinger" audio and ...
    • Soul Shackling Eddy gets cheaper and weaker at the start but not at the end. The three parts cost 30, 10 and 10 Rage and their opening hit shrinks (flat power 79, 59, 39) while the closing slam stays 229 flat at a 136% rate in all three.
    • Coffin swing 3 is a Rage jackpot. One release of swing family 3 gives 50 Rage, almost three times family 1 (18), and it also has the highest flat power (132) and rate (212%) of the four.
    • Backstab and Execution are weak for what they are. His Backstab uses a x1.25 rear multiplier where Terrence's uses x2 (see Backstab), so it hits for about 63% of what the same row would with x2.
    • Execution: Deterrence is a second hit. The talent triggers a sphere (radius 3 m, lasting 0.1 s) using a separate move row (SkillIndex 1108: 150 flat at 136%, Guard Break 999) around Norwood that skips the executed enemy.
    • Resistances are halved a second time by traps. In the lunatic state non-physical trap (environment) damage first uses the already reduced resistance and then halves it again, about -75% in total for Fire, Poison, Arcane and Shadow (Pierce is not reduced by the lunatic stat change, so ...
    • Counter move 105 is probably unused. The data holds a "parry" row (SkillIndex 105, flat 95 and 292 at 250%, 100 Rage) but no Norwood talent unlocks it, no SkillChainData row links to it, and no Norwood code calls it (inferred).
    • Only Liberate drains Rage, and Griminess never decays. Norwood's Rage only drains while he is in the Liberate form (Hero10004_Value:Update checks the form first), and his Griminess has no decay or passive regeneration; the Power-decrement stat is only used by Elena's code.
    • Poise regeneration works for him. He has 240 per mille regeneration (24% of the pool per second), unlike Terrence, so any Poise gained from trophies or the +10 lunatic bonus refills in about four seconds when not being hit.
    • Casual difficulty adds Poise to the player on top of this; see Difficulty.

    Jerold#

    • Marks only come from enhanced moves. The plain Light, Heavy and dodge-attack rows carry no mark at all, and neither do the plain Esquive, Thrust and Echo arts, which only arm the detonation fuse.
    • The first mark does not weaken armour. Each mark after the first lowers the target's maximum Physical Defence by 3.5% of its base value, so six marks give -17.5% (five steps).
    • Detonation damage ignores Defence. Each mark deals ceil(0.3 x Jerold's Physical Attack + 29) when it detonates, to a maximum of six marks; the burst has no damage type, so Defence does not reduce it.
    • Chain length picks the art. Each swing sets a hidden tier: Light hits give tiers 1, 1, 2, 2, 3 and Heavy hits give 6, 7, 8, 8, 9.
    • Dodge attacks are chain starters. Light or Heavy after a dodge sets the chain to "one hit done", so the next press is the second hit.
    • Enhanced Light gains more than enhanced Heavy. At Attack 62 the enhanced Light combo hits for 1.5 to 1.9 times the plain one (81 vs 43 on hit 1), while the enhanced Heavy combo is only 8% to 11% stronger.
    • Arrival has no upkeep except time. Arts are free, but Rage does not grow, grids never decay and Soul Dew still recovers (apart from during the cast).
    • Soul Dew only trickles. It recovers at 0.6 per second but each swing adds 3, so Ordeal (30) is usually available within about ten hits.
    • Rage lost on interruption is small. 10 Rage out of 100, only in the lunatic state, only for the local player's Jerold, and only when he is hit while performing a move that carries armour (a move with no armour value does not trigger the loss).
    • Night's Embrace trophy. With this trophy equipped, any enemy with a mark is treated as a Sendril-type enemy for the "damage vs types" bonuses: if it was human and beast it loses the beast bonus, if it was only one of them it loses both, and the Sendril bonus is applied instead.
    • Wheel of Reversal trophy. Each extreme evasion restores 50 Stamina (DodgeAddEnergy).
    • Shared experience on first join. If your save has no stored Jerold entry, he is created with Terrence's level and experience and with all attribute points unspent (level - 1 points), and every attribute allocation set to zero.
    • Serene fires bolts. The enhanced Serene finishers launch straight-line projectiles from the first trigger of the move: one bolt for the enhanced Begone, Upraising and the first three Serene steps (moves 35-37, 57-59) and for the plain Star Crossed and fourth step (moves 34, 56), two ...
    • Maze availability. In the DLC maze mode he is only enabled if the Tides expansion has been entered and his join quest is complete (inferred from CheckHeroEnable).
    • Cosmetic poses. The Ordeal pose cycles through 5 stances (PoseMax 5) in a fixed rotation; they have no effect on gameplay. The internal name of the move is literally "striking a pose".

    Sendril marks (Griminess)#

    • The mark count cap is 6 but the first mark is weak: only the second to sixth hit change Defence.
    • The detonation is not reduced by Defence; it is flat per mark and scales only with Jerold's Attack stat.
    • The 1-second fuse means that Serene and Soul Sword arts explode their own marks nearly at once; mark build-up for a big payoff needs plain Light and Heavy swings that do not fuse.
    • Marks are removed when they detonate, so a long chain of enhanced swings followed by a single art is the efficient pattern.
    • Lunatic Jerold gains Rage from marked targets only on hits that give Rage at all, so the detonation itself gives none.

    Elena#

    • Power is the ultimate's timer, and it decays by itself. Power starts at 0 every battle.
    • Imperial Will stretches the buff only a little. Each attack, Flash or Parry applies a 5-second status that lowers Power decay by 3 (from 50 to 47 per mille).
    • Impregnable costs Power. The automatic block (single-blade) or dodge (dual-blade) is a skill that needs and spends 15 Power, so one full ultimate pays for at most six automatic saves, minus decay.
    • Rage never decays. Her data has a Rage-decay stat of 60, but the code never applies Rage decay to Elena: only Norwood and Jerold call the decay function.
    • Only attacks with flat power count for the stamina discount. The three Fleeting-line nodes cut Stamina costs once you have chained 3 attacks in a row, but a hit counts only if its flat power is above 0.
    • Blocking works differently from Terrence. Her GuardDamageRatio is 0 (Terrence has 1). While the guard holds, a blocked attack deals no damage even if its Guard Break beats your Guard of 75.
    • Demiurge's Curse is a stagger bomb, and only single-blade moves apply it. The curse is applied by the 5th hit of the single-blade Light chain, the 5th and 6th hits of the single-blade Heavy chain, and the Counter after a Parry (skill 25).
    • The dual-blade Light chain starts weak. Steps 1, 2, 3 and 5 of the chain carry no flat power, just 50 to 80% of your Attack (52 to 83 raw attack at 104). Only steps 4 and 6 hit properly (164).
    • Shadow's Rage costs 34, not 30. The talent text says 30 Rage, but both Shadow's Rage rows have RageSub 34. Hitting with it gives 100 Rage (RageAdd 100), so a hit refills the whole meter.
    • Perfect dodge details. Dual-blade Dodge combo steps 3 and 5 are the Flash: time slows to 0.32x for about 0.35 seconds, Elena gets a Rage tick, and with Flash Return a Stamina tick.
    • Blade of Discipline beat-back is a curse source. If the Blade of Discipline (either version), or the Light-hold switch from dual to single form, catches an enemy attack, there is no slow-down but the enemy receives the Demiurge's Curse status directly.
    • Ranged parry achievement. "Return to Sender" counts ranged attacks deflected by An Eye for an Eye: ten within ten seconds.
    • Demiurge's Might. The talent has a base value of 250 and a step of +100.
    • Dark Mist Morph. Holding Dodge for 0.25 s costs 15 Rage and 48 Stamina to start; releasing costs another 48 Stamina.
    • Potions. Health Potion capacity is 2 (Terrence has 3); the talents Health Potions: Stock and others raise it.
    • Death by falling. Quest 10078001 completes if Elena dies from a fall at one specific spot in Arcade stage 1007 (a coordinate box checked in Hero10007_Value:DamageDrop).

    Attributes#

    • One curve, six attributes. The game never tells you that a point of Strength and a point of Vitality are worth exactly the same on the underlying curve; only the hero's weights decide.
    • Steep up to about 30, flat after 60. The stats are built to reward a spread of roughly 20 to 40 points in each attribute you care about. Moving an attribute from 60 to 99 buys less Attack than 6 points from 20 to 26.
    • Pierce resistance is 5 times the Defence curve. Intellect and Vitality each carry 250% weight, so Pierce resistance climbs five times as fast in total as ordinary Defence (260 at the start against Defence 63 for Terrence).
    • Elemental resistances are always four identical stats. Fire, Poison, Arcane and Dark resistance have the same weights for every hero, so you cannot specialise.
    • Endurance means damage for Jerold and Elena. Their Attack, Crit damage and Crit rate all draw on Endurance; their Strength is a minor source.
    • Poise has no attribute source. Property 13 (Poise) is 0 for every hero at every attribute setting. All Poise comes from Trophies (and +130 on Casual).
    • The hidden attribute decides class identity. Norwood's Rage decay is half of Terrence's (50 against 100 per mille per second), and Jerold's and Elena's is 60.
    • Values above 99 would break the stats. The attribute table has exactly 99 rows per attribute although the code declares a 200 maximum.
    • Leveling cannot overspend. The 98-point total is a hard limit: attribPoints rises by exactly 1 per level-up (playerinfoctrl.lua 10595) and nothing else adds attribute points in the scripts.
    • A leftover system. The BufferData table (12 rows, internal names for "Blessing" and "Source" buffs) carries numbers of 10, 20 or 40 in its attribute columns for six of its entries, the Life, Attack and Defence "Blessing" (rows 1 to 3) and "Source" (rows 7 to 9) buffs; their Chinese descriptions speak of raising a level by 1 or 2.

    Character stats#

    • Stats screen labels that lie. The English label list in the game has "Buff Duration" for stat 9, but the code uses stat 9 as the Rage decay rate; "Exp Bonus" for stat 12, which the code uses as the Power cap; and a label in Chinese for stat 3 that means roughly "swift-strike damage" while the code uses it as Crit resistance.
    • Four dead stats. Mana (7), HP on hit (28), Stamina on crit (29) and Mana on crit (30) are never read by combat code. Mana appears only in the save-slot display and debug tools.
    • Debug leftovers. The debug tool refers to "DropBonus" and "Resist" properties that do not exist in the stat list, which would evaluate to nothing if called.
    • Crit resistance is enemy-only. All heroes have Crit resistance 0, which the code treats as "no effect".
    • Heroes' Resilience is effectively infinite. 10000 maximum plus a regen of 100x maximum per second means a hero's Resilience refills almost as fast as it is drained.
    • Terrence has no Poise regen. His fixed Poise regen is 0 while all other heroes have 240. He has no base Poise either, so this only matters when a Trophy (or Casual mode) gives him some.
    • Elena and Jerold regain stamina later. Their exhaustion value is 92, others 100, so they sit out 0.8 seconds after hitting 0 Stamina.
    • Elena's Power regen is unused. She has a Power regen of 6 per mille but only Jerold's code applies Power regen (inferred from the call sites).
    • The three type flags work like keys. An enemy with the Human flag (38) takes the Atk vs Humanoid bonus, and so on.

    Altars#

    • Altars advance the story. Some altars complete quests when you open them.
    • Opening is saving. The game saves when the altar menu opens, not just when you rest. Closing it without resting still counts as a rest-free visit.
    • Dropped loot vanishes. Because Recovery clears the ground, pick up drops before resting. The same clearing happens when you die. Ordinary ground loot does survive saving and quitting; only a rest or a death removes it.
    • Loading refills potions. When a story scene is loaded (not the very first load, and not inside the maze), every hero's Health Potions are set to maximum again, so reloading a save is another free potion refill.
    • Merchant stock. Limited stock on items flagged "refresh" returns to maximum whenever you rest or die, not on a timer.
    • Level and Dedication. The experience bar and all unlocked bones show in the Dedication tab; at level 99 the tab refuses further spending. Details in Leveling.
    • Trophy protections. Resting clears and re-applies the No Death, Shield and Small Shield trophy statuses, so those effects are renewed by a rest (whether they also renew on other occasions is not covered here).
    • Facing rule. The altar test combines squared distance with a dot product on your facing direction, so you can be standing right next to an altar and still get no button if you are facing away from it.

    Leveling#

    • One shared level. Spending bones levels up every hero in the save, including heroes you have not unlocked yet. The code adds the same experience to every entry in the hero list.
    • Experience resets on each level. The bar always runs from 0 to the next row of HeroExp. Experience is never cumulative inside the save data.
    • Attribute respec. Resetting attributes (see Attributes) sets every hero's free points to level minus 1 and clears all spent points. It runs through a dialog option that consumes one Starry Honey Wine.
    • Per-attribute cap. Attribute values cannot exceed 99 even if you have spare points.
    • Dead rows. The experience table has 500 rows, but the game refuses levels above 99.
    • Health Potion refill. Dying or resting also refills the Health Potion charges of every hero to their maximum (Terrence 3, the other playable heroes 2, plus any talent bonus). See Altars.
    • Both heroes must fall. The penalty only applies when no other hero in your party is alive. If your second hero survives, the game swaps to them and takes nothing.
    • New Game Plus keeps levels. The restart routine removes only items flagged "GamePlusRemove" and resets story progress.
    • Challenge and Arcade presets. Starting a challenge hero (challenge menu) or an Arcade hero calls a routine that sets every hero's level to the preset HeroLevel of that challenge or Arcade hero, gives the chosen hero the preset attribute list and (unless told not to) maxes that hero's talents.
    • Level-up tutorial. The first time you reach level 2 a special tutorial pops up.

    Benita talent tree#

    • Four free nodes. Touch of Whip, Backstab, Execution and Reclaiming the Pure Blood start at level 1. Counter Attack, Whip of Monstrance and the rest each cost a Chipped Immundus.
    • Rhythm Attack is secretly required for the strengthened moves. Seven moves in the skill table carry SkillTalent = 2, meaning two separate nodes must cover them: the four combos of move 4 (the heavy triple, covered by Dance of Whip and by Rhythm Attack) and moves 28, 30 and 32 ...
    • Soul Kiss swaps statuses. The bloodsucking status 10013 (parameter 800, 6 s) becomes 10014 (1000, 9 s) and 10015 (1200, 12 s).
    • Elixir Research stacks on a 40% base. The blessing roll succeeds if a roll of 1-1000 is below 401 + the bonus, so the base chance is 40%, then 70% and 100%.
    • Wind Whisper and Holy Soul are status swaps too. Wind Whisper's stamina regeneration (status 10019, 40 s) becomes 10020 (50 s) then 10021 (60 s). Holy Soul's shield (10016, strength 200) becomes 10017 (300) then 10018 (400).
    • Lunatic Benita pays more for elixir moves. Moves that use Power cost 33% extra. Adapt to Abnormality: Elixir shrinks that surcharge by 33.3% then 66.6% (to about 22% and 11%).
    • Potion resistance. While lunatic, a potion also applies a 30 s resistance status that halves further healing and the strength of potion-type effects.
    • Bloodthirsty Instinct sits on a built-in bonus. Lunatic Benita already deals +15% damage below 50% Health. Below 30% the talent doubles it to +30%.
    • Optimized Tubes adds 10% to the Power gauge, and Shunt Tubes adds 10 Power per Backstab or Execution (the game text speaks of "maximum number of Elixirs").
    • Long Distance Counterattack only switches on the parry hit box for her normal attacks (ParryUse); there is no number.

    Talents#

    • **The Condition column decides what a node touches.** For move effects it is a list of move,combo pairs. For item effects it is a list of item ids or item types, for stat effects a stat id.
    • Text and data can disagree. Descriptions are built from TextValue, effects from Value.
    • Lucky Madness does not use percent. It adds 5 or 10 to the weight of drop entries, and the code that rolls drops does not test for the lunatic state at all (inferred).
    • Lunatic nodes are modifiers of built-in effects, not new effects: they shrink a penalty or multiply a bonus that each hero already has. See Lunatic state.
    • Benita's blessing talent is global. The blessing roll reads Benita's talents directly, so Elixir Research helps even when you play someone else (see Blessing).
    • Unused feature: groups. The code supports talent groups (TalentParent, type 999) where children share a parent's level, but no row in the shipped table uses it.
    • Elena's Sanity nodes are not bought. Seven of them are granted by defeating the seven Obsessed Ones in Arcade mode (each monster row names a talent to add) and are restored in later runs through the achievements for those fights.
    • Strange messages. The "not enough stone" messages use the labels "Not Enough Immundus" for both the Immundus and the Crystal stone and the bare text "Immundus Crystal" for the Diamond stone; this is a text-table oddity.
    • Debug switch. A developer flag (DebugTalentOpenAll) forces every node to at least level 1; it is off in the shipped game.

    Terrence talent tree#

    • Level 1 is enough to continue. The tree check only asks that the previous node has any level above 0. You can pass through the cheap first levels of a chain and spend the expensive Diamond levels later.
    • Three nodes are free. Lunge Attack, Parry and Execution start at level 1. Terrence does not start with Backstab or Rising Slash: each costs one Chipped Immundus.
    • Level N always uses the Nth stone type of the node. There is no way to pay a level with a different stone, and the cost is shown in the confirm box ("Consume a ...").
    • Lunatic-state nodes scale the built-in penalty, not a flat number. In the Lunatic state Terrence loses 30% max Health and gains 10% attack (both relative to base).
    • Lucky Madness is a drop weight, not a percentage. The maze and story drop tables carry two spare columns (ProbAdd1 = 5, ProbAdd2 = 10).
    • Revenge scales with the hit. It gives 50% of the attacker's own "Rage on hit" value. Most enemy moves carry 6, so a typical hit gives about 3 Rage; moves with 0 give nothing.
    • Furor and the poise nodes add flat points. Furor adds Rage per hit to 12 listed moves; Knight's Tenacity (+15) and Rising Slash: Tenacity (+38) add to the move's own poise value; Rising Slash: Inhibition adds +20 to the poise damage dealt.
    • Parry: Enhanced reduces the stamina lost when a block lands through the parry move (the formula multiplies the stamina cost by 0.7). It does not change the parry window.
    • Devout multiplies the Sanity drained by your own hits by 0.85. Sanity is only drained by hits in story mode, outside the Dark Forest scenes (see Sanity).
    • Sanction: Broken Sin boosts two moves. The node's condition lists Sanction (move 8) and Discipline (move 9).

    Viola talent tree#

    • Starts with six nodes. Light Combo, Powerful Shooting, Rapid-Fire, Counter Attack, Backstab and Execution are all open from the start.
    • Break Out only exists with the talent. After a Backstab or Execution the game looks for a status swap; without the node nothing is applied.
    • Bloody multiplies crit rate by 1.2 against bleeding targets. With a 20% crit rate that is 24%, not 40%.
    • Condemn changes which Bleed Viola applies. Her moves that apply Bleed (status 10034, 10 s) apply 10035 (15 s) at level 1 and 10036 (20 s) at level 2.
    • Bullets Recycling has a base of 10%. Whenever Viola is the leading hero, every kill already has a 10% chance of dropping 1 special bullet; the talent turns it into 20% for 2 and 30% for 3.
    • Improved Magazine raises the special bullet cap from 12 to 14, 16 or 18.
    • Lucky Shooter rolls once per bullet spent: on a 20% success the shot is free.
    • Adapt to Abnormality: Critical Strike works on the built-in lunatic bonus (+10% of base crit damage). The talent multiplies that bonus by 2 at level 1 and by 3 at level 2, so the real result is +20% and +30%.
    • Adapt to Abnormality: Stamina acts on a built-in penalty. While lunatic and carrying the "Wounded (Gunner)" status (id 10004, internal name), exhaustion recovery is slowed by 50%.
    • Brutality inflicts true Bleed (status 10001) with a 20% chance per critical hit, only in the lunatic state.
    • Devout multiplies the Sanity drained by your own hits by 0.85.
    • Lucky Madness adds drop weight to item types 801 (Raw Stone) and 803 (Blackwater materials); there is no Sanity check in the drop code (inferred).

    Elena talent tree#

    • Where the unlock comes from. Each of seven Arcade monsters carries a TalentAdd field in StoryMonster: Heggem's gives Shadow Moon (talent 17401), Adamina Path of Suffering (17402), Exilium Fleeting (17403), Edith Demiurge's Wish (17405), Karst Cave Path of Truth (17407), Katib Eternal War (17404), Blessedland Finale (17406).
    • Only Elena triggers it. The in-run unlock, and the achievement call that sits beside it in the same function, fire only when the controlled hero is Elena (10007).
    • Re-granted at stage select. Whenever an Arcade run opens at stage select, every Sanity talent whose achievement is done is set to level 1 for Elena.
    • Arcade starts maxed. Starting an Arcade run with Elena (the default Arcade hero) raises every talent outside the Sanity page to max level (AddTalentToMaxWithOutSan).
    • Stamina discounts stack. Fleeting (15%), Eternal War (15%) and Demiurge's Wish (20%) all use the same effect, and the game sums them.
    • The descriptions overstate. Fleeting is called "slightly" and Eternal War just "reduces", yet both are 15%.
    • Demiurge's Might uses Rage thresholds. The bonus is +25% to weakness-bar damage (the game text calls it resilience reduction) above 33% Rage and +35% above 66% Rage, only for single-blade attacks (moves 1, 3, 4, 5, 24, 25) and only when Elena is the player-controlled hero.
    • Demiurge's Curse grows by switching statuses. Each level applies a different status instead of raising one stack cap: level 1 uses status 10115 (3 stacks, 10 s), level 2 uses 10116 (5 stacks, 11 s), level 3 uses 10117 (5 stacks, 12 s).
    • Flash Return restores stamina through a 0.1 second status. The talent turns on status 10118 for a successful Flash dodge.
    • Imperial Will and Impregnable work together. Imperial Will lengthens the Imperial Ambition / King's Decree buff, while Impregnable converts that buff into auto-dodges (Ambition) or auto-blocks (Decree), spending buff time per hit.
    • No Sanity. Elena's tree has no node that depends on the Abnormal State; her page 4 is the achievement bonuses above.

    Jerold talent tree#

    • Less Rage is the strongest node. It subtracts a share from the Rage cost of 28 move variants: 16.5%, 33% or 49.5%. At level 3 a 30-Rage Soul Sword move costs about 15.
    • Bouncy starts at the third hit. The 30% stamina discount applies only once your current combo has landed 3 attacks.
    • Calming Rage in numbers. Being interrupted in the Abnormal State costs 10 Rage. Level 1 makes that 7.5, level 2 makes it 5.
    • Sendril Affinity in numbers. The Abnormal State already gives +10% Rage on attacks against Sendril-marked enemies.
    • Sendril Blast applies a status. The talent makes Backstab and Execution apply status 10103, which the code calls TalentDarkClearStatusId (its Sendril-mark role is inferred from the name).
    • Lucky Madness really boosts trophies. The text says "combat items", but the node's data targets trophy types 601 to 603.
    • Esquive has one level. Its description mentions leveling up for a longer dodge, but the node has only a single level.
    • Enhanced Elixir mismatch. The text says 10 Sanity; the data and code use 5.
    • Sendril's Arrival: Brier is narrow. The reserve only triggers when you counter with the Special block while Sendril's Arrival is active and Jerold is in the Abnormal State.
    • Costs are not smooth. A few nodes skip the middle stones: Less Rage costs Immundus, then a Sea Tooth Bone, then a Diamond.

    Norwood talent tree#

    • How stones really work. Each level eats exactly one stone of the type named for that level, and a node can be bought whenever its "After" node is at level 1 or higher; it does not need to be maxed (inferred from the tile logic in the talent screen).
    • Effects add up. The game sums the values of every node with the same effect type and matching move. Several of Norwood's numbers therefore stack with the hero's built-in values rather than replace them.
    • Shield of Griminess is +5, not a multiplier. A perfectly timed guard already gives 12 Griminess. The talent adds 5, for 17 per timed block. The same block also refills 30% of his stamina bar regardless of the talent.
    • Absorbing Griminess is not tied to damage. Despite its description, the game scales the bonus from a per-attack value on the incoming skill (PowerAddHit): level 1 adds 50% of it, level 2 adds 100%.
    • Devout is 15% and hits your own damage output. The Sanity drained from Norwood is proportional to the damage he deals, and Devout cuts it by 15%.
    • Adapt to Abnormality: Inertia is stronger than it reads. The abnormal state normally lowers fire, poison, arcane and dark resistance by 50%.
    • Berserker Instinct never works on bosses. It rolls a 30% chance, only on a few specific hits (combo 3 or 5 of the code's HP skill family and combo 3 of its LP family), only if the enemy is not blocking the hit or is hit from behind, and only against enemies that are not bosses.
    • Obstinate stacks with the base bonus. The Abnormal State already gives Norwood +10 resilience; Obstinate makes it +15.
    • Lucky Madness is a weight, not a percentage. Every trophy entry in an enemy's or a maze's drop table has a second and third weight (nearly always +5 and +10).
    • Enhanced Elixir mismatch. The text says 10 Sanity per potion; the number in the data and used by the code is 5.
    • Free Execution, paid Backstab. Execution is a free starting node for every hero, but only Norwood has to buy his Backstab.

    Items, trophies and crafting#

    Consumables, trophies, reforging, alchemy and merchants.

    Consumables#

    • Potions cannot be wasted on drops. When your potion stock is full, the Health Potion entry is removed from every drop pool before the roll, and a Health Potion on the ground cannot be picked up ("bag full").
    • The first kill is a guaranteed refill for four enemies in Heggem; every other regular enemy has the 35% pool.
    • Potion strength is a flat 35% of the base maximum Health, so potions are relatively stronger the lower your maximum Health, and equipment that raises max Health does not raise the heal.
    • Only one buff per family. Drinking a different resistance elixir cancels the previous one, and Elixir of Stamina and Elixir of Energy replace each other. Details in Buffs.
    • Revive key rules. The Ancient Silver Key only works if your second hero is dead; otherwise the game shows an error and keeps the key. It does nothing in dream stories.
    • The Broken Bronze Whistle is not free. Using it runs the same "lost bones" routine as death: you lose a random 25% to 35% of your Bone Fragments (the fractional part rounds up with a 30% chance).
    • Maze-only and non-maze items are enforced. The Elixir of Sanity is refused in the maze, and Hound Amulet, Glowstone, Vertebra Nail and the Moon Jellyfish Jar are refused outside the maze and Arcade mode.
    • Consumables cannot be sold. Their price entries are 0 or -1 (the Bone Whistle has 50, but it is a consumable) and the sell screen only lists materials and bone items. See Items.
    • Talents. Prolonged Duration (+10/20/30% duration) and Improved Effect (+10/20/30% strength) cover most elixirs; Sanity: Enhanced Elixir restores 10 Sanity per potion according to its text, but the data value is 5 (see the talent trees).
    • Doppelganger. A sanity doppelganger drinks your Health Potions; the Health Potion stock lost this way is dropped again when you kill it (the sanity doppelganger enemy; the drop pool exists, the rest is inferred).
    • Prices rise with every cycle. The same shop row has four prices, one per game cycle (an Elixir of Health costs 10 / 15 / 25 / 35 Bone Fragments), and cycles 4 and above use the last one. Placed pick-ups also grow.

    Items#

    • Four of everything. Shop rows, item drops and fixed pick-ups each store four values and the game picks the one that matches the cycle (never above 4). In the first cycle the later columns are never seen.
    • Unreachable items. The data contains items that no shop, drop pool or pick-up gives: Medium Health Potion (no effect), Large Health Potion, the "Revive+" item (21027), and the "Frog" item (an internal placeholder with no English name).
    • Documents are items too. 115 of the 218 materials are readable documents, one copy each. They have no use apart from their text and their quest triggers.
    • The Egg of the Bearer is a hidden beginner item. While it is in your bag, all damage you take is halved (Casual difficulty multiplies it by 0.65 on top, for 0.325 in total).
    • Maze copies. Items 72111-72120 are the maze's own copies of blessed elixirs (and of Hound Amulet and Slightly Sweet Roots+). They do not get the item talents that raise strength or duration. See Consumables.
    • Blessing is item id +1000. A blessed version is simply the id with 1000 added; the game builds it from the plain item this way when a blessing succeeds.

    Throwables#

    • Trophies that change your attack or health do not touch throwables. Conditional attack bonuses (full or half Health), life steal and the self-damage trade-off, and the energy gain on critical hits, all check "no thrown item" in the code.
    • Blocked hits still apply the status. A status is only skipped when the hit was blocked and the status has its guard flag set; Burn, Bleed, Poison, Blind and the weakness drain do not have it, so enemies that block still catch fire.
    • Poison, Burn and Bleed throwables use their own status rows. They do not use the enemy versions of the statuses, so their values (40 per second, 15 s) differ from what enemies use.
    • From the second cycle, Throwing Knives turn into Huge Fangs. The fixed Throwing Knife pick-ups in the first five regions exist only in cycle 1, and the Huge Fang appears in their place from cycle 2 on.
    • The Arcade copies (30012, 30013) are identical in power to 30002 and 30004 and are used only in Arcade stages. See Arcade mode.
    • Ten of each is a hard cap. The bag will not take more; the pick-up message says the bag is full.
    • Stun Crystal's big weakness hit is for regular enemies only. Its boss value is 10, the same as most throwables, so against a boss it is only a 40-damage item.

    Bone Fragment#

    • Spend or dedicate before risky fights. Bone Fragments are the only currency at risk, so dedicating them at an Altar or converting them into Immundus stones or materials is the only way to make them safe.
    • Doubling. A Potion of Wealth doubles every dedication item you pick up for 10 minutes (status 10069, 600 s; the item text also says 10 minutes), so drink it before a farm run, not after.
    • No sell-back loop. A Big Bone sells for 7 Bone Fragments but is worth 200 XP when dedicated, which is 8 times a Bone Fragment's 25. Selling it and dedicating the fragments gives 175 XP, about 12% less.
    • Always auto-collected. Bone Fragments are picked up automatically on contact, no button needed (logic/item/itemscene.lua:115-130: every main type 4 item is auto-get).
    • The Broken Bronze Whistle costs Bone Fragments too. Using the whistle (item 26001) to be carried back to the nearest Horse Bell runs the same loss routine as dying, HostPlayerDeadDrop (ControllerStoryMode:Transfer), so you pay 25-35% of your Bone Fragments for the trip.

    Currencies#

    • Death costs Bone Fragments only. When you die, the game takes between 25% and 35% of your Bone Fragments (a random fraction, rounded down, plus a 30% chance to round up when there is a remainder).
    • The Potion of Wealth doubles the four sizes. Its status (10069, lasting 600 s = 10 minutes, as the item text says) doubles the number picked up of any dedication item.
    • Auto-pickup. The four dedication items are always collected automatically; you never need the pickup button, and no auto-pickup trophy is required (ItemScene treats main type 4 as auto-get and attach).
    • Selling needs a price. The sell list only shows materials and dedication items whose price is above -1, so key stones, documents, formulas and chests never appear (exchangectrl.lua line 752).
    • Shop stock resets differ. Rows marked IsRefresh restock after you respawn; other rows have a hard stock that resets only when a new cycle starts; NotReset rows (Viola's and the Dark Mist shop) never reset, so one-time buys stay bought.
    • Cycle cap. Shop prices and amounts index four columns; the game clamps the cycle at 4 (GameLoopMax), so NG+3, NG+4 and later all use the same numbers.
    • Anthozoan Bones sink. Nine Katib quests (55056, 55058, 55060, 55062, 55064, 55066, 55068, 55084, 55086) each delete one Melos Bottle trophy, one Condensation Honey and a growing number of Anthozoan Bones: 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 210, 240 and 270.

    Dream Chest#

    • Level 6 is rare except in chest 5. Chest 4 gives a level-6 trophy only 6% of the time, chest 5 gives it 42% of the time.
    • The levels shown are trophy levels, not +N. A "level 6" trophy is the maximum enhancement (+5); see Trophy enhancement.
    • The odds are fixed per chest, not per chapter. Your bonus drop-rate talents do not change them, since the roll is a shop action rather than an enemy drop.
    • The roll is deterministic per save. GetRandomTrophyId seeds the random generator with the save's stored seed plus a counter (FS_SaveStory:GetRandomSeed: a seed between 10000 and 99999 plus a counter that grows by 10 with every seeded draw and wraps after 999,999).
    • No restock limit. The rows have unlimited stock (-1) and never reset, so you can buy as many as you can afford and as many as the 600-trophy cap allows.

    Immundus#

    • You cannot max everyone in one run. The visible supply in the first cycle (ground + merchants + Dark Mist stages) is about 64 Chipped, 64 Immundus, 31 Crystal, 12 Diamond and 19 Sea Tooth Bone, against a full five-hero bill of 26 / 86 / 81 / 28 / 19 (Elena's Arcade tree is separate).
    • Shop prices rise every cycle. The same stone costs about 3.3 times as much in cycle 4 as in cycle 1 (20 to 65, 50 to 165).
    • In Dance of the Throne (Arcade) stones turn into time. When you pick up a stone in Arcade mode, it is swapped for a hidden "time reduction" item (ids 72001-72004, internal Chinese names only) that shortens your run clock by 30 s (Chipped), 60 s, 90 s or 120 s (Diamond) per stone.
    • Lucky Madness does not help. Each hero's talent 405 Lucky Madness adds 5 / 10 to the weight of drop entries (fields ProbAdd1/ProbAdd2) while the hero is in the Abnormal state, but only for item types named in the talent (Terrence 701 and 706, Viola 801 and 803, Benita 802 and 702, Norwood and Jerold 601-603).
    • Not sellable. Stones have price -1, so the merchant sell screen never lists them.
    • Limited stack. A stack holds 99; extra pickups are lost and the game prints "bag is full" (AddBagItem).

    Key items#

    • New Game Plus wipes most key items. At the start of a new cycle, every item flagged GamePlusRemove is deleted (FS_ItemList:Restart), (the Egg of the Bearer is also cleared explicitly; see New Game Plus).
    • Lamp progress is kept. Because the lamps and oils are not flagged for removal, your best lamp stays with you across cycles.
    • Condensation Honey seals the Melos Bottles. Nine Katib quests (55056 to 55086) each remove one Melos Bottle trophy, one Condensation Honey and 30, 60, ...
    • Starry Honey Wine is an attribute reset. It sits in the Quest item class (main type 1, id 20029, stack of 10) and survives New Game Plus, but it is the way to respec: it resets your hero's attributes (item text).
    • Where the descriptions come from. The English text is merged from the game's text files; two Arcade items (Key to the Confessional, Pascal's Wager) still have Chinese description text.
    • Two items called Patrick's Ring. Item 10006 is the story prop.

    Materials#

    • Drops are weighted rolls, and an enemy rolls several times. An enemy's DropId is a list such as "10001;10014;10020"; each id is a separate drop group, rolled independently.
    • Full healing charges make materials more likely. Entries that give health-potion charges (item type 201) are removed from the roll while your Health Potion count is already at its maximum (talent bonuses to the maximum count are included).
    • First kill is special. If an enemy has a first-kill list and its quest flag is not yet set, that list replaces the normal list for the first kill only.
    • A second set of drops appears when you are unsteady. While your hero is in a non-normal sanity state (Abnormal or Lunatic), enemies also roll their separate SanDropId lists. See Sanity and Lunatic state.
    • Lucky Madness talent. Talent 405 adds 5 (level 1) or 10 (level 2) to the weight of drop rows of the item types it covers, while you are Abnormal: Terrence 701 and 706, Viola 801 and 803, Benita 802 and 702, Norwood and Jerold 601 to 603 (the trophy types, although Jerold's own text says "combat items").
    • New Game Plus shifts the weights. Drop rows have four columns, one for each cycle (capped at 4).
    • Shops mark parts up heavily. The Heggem merchant sells Broken Arrow, Broken Rod and Broken Bone Spur (3 of each) at 15 / 25 / 35 / 50 Bone Fragments per cycle, but a merchant buys them for 2. Do not sell parts you will need.
    • Orphan and unobtainable items. Nitrate Powder (70064), needed twice for the Fireworks Barrel, has no drop, pickup, shop or quest source in the data (inferred: obtained another way, or cut).
    • No dedication value. Only the four bone items turn into experience at an Altar; parts, herbs and stones never do. See Currencies.

    Trophies#

    • The cap on what you can hold is 600 trophies including the six equipped ones. When the bag is full, pickups, chest purchases and automatic collection are refused.
    • Equipped trophies are not per-hero. One loadout serves all heroes; the bonuses go to the hero on screen.
    • Level and stats are table lookups. The trophy keeps its original item id when enhanced; its level is stored separately and shifts each property id by one grade step per level.
    • Death-bound trophies. Six pots are destroyed on death (effect 114, see Trophy list).
    • The "second quality" column is dead data: 1,050 property rows carry a SecondaryValue of 0.
    • Drop cap by cycle. New Game Plus raises loot levels only until cycle 4.
    • Eight trophies cannot be obtained: item ids 67068 to 67075 are placeholders with untranslated developer labels.

    Trophy list#

    • New Game Plus raises the level of the loot. For 33 of the fixed-stat trophies the game keeps one item row per level (for example Truth is 60025, 60225, 60325 and 60425).
    • Some stock does not scale. Merchant 1902 sells six trophies (Brass Ring of Handel 4, Figurine of Sorrowful Nun 5, Shimmering Shard 6, The Rose 10, Fanatical Sand 15, Brooch with Sacred Pattern 20 Dominator Crystals, one in stock each) as the +0 item in every cycle, even though +1 to +3 rows exist.
    • Item ids encode the level. The +1 to +3 copies add 200, 300 or 400 to the +0 id for most trophies (60025 to 60225); the "destroyed on death" family steps by 10 (61011, 61021, 61031, 61041, 61051, 61061).
    • Ten Melos Bottles, one slot. The ten Melos Bottles (60068 to 60075, 60079, 60080) share one equip-group id, so only one of them can be worn at a time.
    • The Melos Bottles are an upgrade ladder. Between two bottles the quest chain runs a "delete item" step (quest ids 55056, 55058, 55060, 55062, 55064, 55066, 55068, 55084, 55086) that takes away the bottle you hold, 1 Condensation Honey and 30 x N Anthozoan Bones (30, 60, 90, 120, 150 ...
    • Unique and undiscardable trophies. 57 of the 94 fixed-stat trophies have Price -1, which blocks discarding them and keeps them off the vendor sell screens.
    • Death-bound trophies. Those six pots have effect 114: they are removed from your equipment when the player dies (the code runs FS_TrophyEquipList:Dead when the secondary host player is dead).
    • Eight placeholder trophies. Item ids 67068 to 67075 still carry Chinese developer labels instead of English names ("attack up while Abnormal", "defence up at full HP" and so on), have developer comments instead of descriptions, and appear in no drop, shop, quest or chest table.
    • Two effect types exist only in code. TrophyLogicType also defines 108 (damage per second) and 117 (dead drop sub), but no trophy in the data uses them. (inferred: cut effects)
    • Strength units. The data's developer comments describe the parameter of effect types 121 to 126 as per-mille (thousandths), so 125 / 170 / 200 would read as 12.5% / 17% / 20% (inferred; the exact use is on Trophy effects).

    Trophy sources#

    • Region progress raises the level. Enemy drops use per-region groups, and the same trophy is listed at a higher level in later regions. You do not need New Game Plus to see +1 or +2 versions.
    • New Game Plus raises it again. Merchants, pickups and drop groups hold one trophy id per cycle (the cycle is capped at 4), so 33 trophies are given at +0, +1, +2 or +3 by cycle.
    • First-kill guarantees. Two groups give a trophy with 100% chance the first time: the female butcher in Adamina (Blood-stained Scissors) and the boar knight (group 50016) in Katib (Strange Gall Bladder).
    • One trophy per Dream Chest purchase. The shop code rolls once for the whole purchase and the bag routine creates a single trophy, but the price is charged for the full quantity.
    • The roll is seeded. The chest uses the save's random seed plus a counter that grows by 10 per call, not the clock.
    • Bag limit. The bag holds up to 600 trophies (equipped ones count). A full bag blocks chest purchases, pickups and auto-collection.
    • Unlinked drop groups. Groups 40007 (Edith) and 60007 (Blessedland) hold trophies, but no enemy row names them (inferred: boss or event drops set outside these tables).

    Trophy effects#

    • The "Half" in the data names is not 50% for rage. Every rage check uses 30%. Attack and defence "Half" effects do use 50%.
    • Self-damage trophies cannot kill you, and full-HP trophies shut off the instant you lose a single HP: the check is current >= max.
    • Defence boosts of 450% are strong but capped by the damage floor. With the 30% floor of the damage formula, the 127 and 128 trophies cut hits by roughly 30% to 60% while you are at full HP (see the worked examples above); weak hits already near the floor gain the least.
    • A 500 shield plus a 250 shield gives you 250. Resting adds the 500-point status first and the 250-point status second, and each shield status resets the pool to zero before filling it, so wearing Basilisk's Protection or Halo Pendant together with Double-star Brooch leaves you with a ...
    • Shields and death wards are only renewed at altars. The Lua code has no other source (the game engine could still add them elsewhere, inferred), so a half-used ward or shield stays as it is until you rest again.
    • Loneliness Whisper is a flat 50% sanity reduction, not a chance, despite its description.
    • Melos Bottles change the sanity state without touching sanity. Equipped, they keep the Abnormal-state bonuses permanently active, while your real sanity bar can still sink into the Lunatic state.
    • Self-damage eats shields first. Discipline, Shimmering Shard and Brooch with Sacred Pattern wear down the skill shield and then the trophy shield you earned by resting before they touch your HP.
    • Life-steal and self-damage ignore damage over time. Only direct skill hits count; bleed, poison and burn ticks do not, and every hit gives or costs at least 1 HP because of the rounding up.
    • Wheel of Reversal is Jerold-only, and Night's Embrace is Jerold-only in practice because it needs his mark.
    • Auto-collect respects the 600-trophy cap, so a full trophy bag is the only thing it refuses for trophies.
    • Enhancement leaves effect numbers alone. Only stat lines get bigger.
    • Unused content. Effect 108 (lose a fraction of maximum HP every second) is fully coded but no trophy has it. Effect 117 exists only as a name. Effect 126 exists only on a test row.
    • Two descriptions contradict the code. "Reduced chance to lose sanity" is a flat halving, and "HP below 50%" for rage is really 30%.

    Trophy enhancement#

    • There is no failure, no gold cost and no sacrificial trophy. Enhancement is fully deterministic. The sacrifice mechanic belongs to reforging, which uses no stones.
    • The sub slot is locked. An old design where a second trophy was fed into enhancement still exists in the menu code (it lists same-type, same-level trophies), but the click is blocked in stone mode, so it cannot be used (inferred: a leftover of an earlier design).
    • The cap is +5 because of one code constant, not because the stones run out. TrophyStrengthLevelMax = 6 hides trophies at internal level 6 from the list.
    • Enhancing an equipped trophy changes your stats immediately because the equipment total is recalculated right after each step.
    • Melos Bottles, the Marcion Puzzle Ball and "destroyed on death" trophies cannot be enhanced, even though they are fixed-stat items. The code refuses them through the data flag alone (EmotionType is 1 for them).
    • A found trophy's level is stored on its own and also hinted by its item id. Saves keep a separate "Level" value for each trophy, and the pre-enhanced item rows carry the same level in their data (see above).

    Random trophy properties#

    • Rolled at pickup, then frozen. The roll happens when the trophy object is created in your inventory (a drop, a reward or a merchant purchase).
    • The level does not change the roll. Each "+N" version is a separate item whose property ids already point at the +N pool rows, so the pool comes from the item id, not from enhancing (which these trophies cannot do anyway).
    • Save-scumming does not help (inferred from the code). The seed and the roll counter are saved with your story. If you reload a save and pick up the same trophies in the same order, they should roll exactly the same values.
    • New seed when the maze is cleared. Choosing to return to Mist Camp after the last maze floor (the prompt about the Terror Obsessions being dispersed) rolls a brand-new 5-digit seed (10000-99999).
    • The bottom of each range is unreachable. For example, an Attack +0 pool row reads 20-32, but the lowest possible roll is 21. Every value in between is equally likely (one uniform draw per property).
    • Consistency check. A developer test save embedded in the game code holds a Raw Gem Amulet +5 (69507) with Attack 93, Crit Rate 14.5% and Crit Damage 14.8%, and Stag Rings +5 (69515) with Stamina 114, Stamina Regen 126 and Crit Rate 12.8%.
    • Stats stack normally. Random values go into the same stat totals as fixed trophy stats and reforged sub-properties.

    Trophy reforge cycles#

    • The tier, not the category, sets the cycle. Families 10-12 are offense, 20-22 defense and 30-32 endurance, so a Scars trophy with a family-21 first property (Double-star Brooch) reforges into defensive stats.
    • Broadly, tiers 11x give the lowest values, 10x the middle and 12x the highest (the ranges overlap, so compare the table). Tier 123, 223 and 323 are the best tier of their cycle.
    • The sacrifice's level matters about as much as the tier.
    • Tiers 101 and 111 step unevenly for Atk vs Humanoid/Beasts/Sendril: tier 101 goes +2.5%, +1.5%, +1.5%, +1.5%, +2.5% (6% to 15.5%) and tier 111 goes +2.5% then +1.5% four times (4.5% to 13%).
    • Tier 122 is the only tier where Atk vs Sendril (8% to 18%) differs from Atk vs Humanoid and Atk vs Beasts (9% to 21.5%), most likely a data entry error (Atk vs Sendril rows 12421-12426).
    • Health in the defense cycle grows faster than linearly at low tiers (e.g. tier 200: 35/60/90/125/165/210). That makes a +5 sacrifice especially valuable there.

    Trophy reforging#

    • It is a cycle, not a lottery. After the first reforge the result is fully predictable: one step along the trophy's effective cycle, in the direction set by the sacrificed category.
    • Cheap steering, one expensive finish. Only the last sacrifice decides the value. Walk the sub-property to the entry just before the stat you want using +0 junk trophies, then make the final step with a +5 trophy.
    • No duplicates. Stats the trophy already has as main properties are skipped. A trophy with 4 main stats in its cycle (e.g. Figurine of Sorrowful Nun, Brooch with Sacred Pattern) only flips between two values.
    • The first roll uses the clock. The 1 to 10 random steps come from a generator that is reseeded on every call from the system clock (the last six digits of the Unix time in seconds, reversed), not from the save's seed.
    • The sacrifice counts at its current level. The value uses the sacrificed trophy's level as it is now (GetLevel), so a trophy you enhanced to +5 counts as +5, exactly like a trophy that dropped as +5.
    • No hidden modifiers. The reforge code reads only the main trophy's own data, its current sub-property, the sacrifice's category and the sacrifice's level.
    • Sub-properties are permanent across New Game+ (inferred). The New Game+ item purge (FS_ItemList:Restart) only removes flagged bag items and does not touch the trophy list, so reforged trophies keep their sub-property.
    • The cycle belongs to the stat, not the category. Some Scars trophies use the defense cycle (Double-star Brooch) and some Resistance and Nightmare trophies use the offense or endurance cycle; see Trophy reforge cycles.
    • Main level is irrelevant. A +0 trophy reforged with a +5 sacrifice gets the same sub-property as a +5 trophy reforged with a +5 sacrifice. Reforge order relative to enhancement does not matter.
    • Equipped trophies can be reforged in the main slot, and your stats update at once. An equipped trophy can never be the sacrifice.
    • Crit Rate and Crit Damage exist in two cycles (offense and endurance) with slightly different values. Both add to the same stat.

    Alchemy#

    • Unlocked recipes are permanent. Every quest id used by ShowQuestId is flagged "not reset" in StoryQuest, so a recipe you have unlocked survives deaths and New Game Plus restarts, and the formula shops do not restock either (NotReset = 1, IsRefresh = 0 ...
    • Crafting is deterministic. No failure chance, no bonus output, no hidden quality. The only dice near alchemy are in Blessing.
    • A batch is capped by the stack of 10, not by your materials, for every elixir and combat item.
    • Slot 3 of every trophy recipe is the Raw Stone. Slot 2 is the "other part". The screen has code that replaces the name and icon of slot 2 with "???" until the quest of that ingredient is done.
    • Any-of unlocks. Crazy Mask appears once you obtain either the Minion Mask or the Noble Hat; Mother's Love with the Tattered Puppet or the Bloody Bandage; Father's Love with Virgo's Whip or the Warm Flesh; Seal of the Pious with the Broken Blessed Tome or the Sea Monster's Scales; Cocoon of Deceit with the Moist Sludge or the Broken Carapace.
    • The Tristram and Basilisk trophies form a 2 x 3 grid. The three Tristram trophies need Bloodstained Tristram plus one of Sanctus Bell, Eucharist or Cruets; the three Basilisk trophies need Bloodstained Basilisk plus the same three.
    • Equip groups. Eternity and Broken Eternity share one equip-group id (60040), so only one of the two can be worn.
    • Making things is counted. Crafting a combat item, an elixir or a trophy each feeds its own achievement counter, and the first craft of anything awards a "first time" achievement.
    • Crafted trophies are never "unique" trophies. Unique trophies (a UniqueId above 0) refuse a second copy; none of the 39 crafted trophies has one, so you can make as many as the bag allows.

    Blessing#

    • The tutorial overstates the reward. The popup says blessing raises the potion's effect, may reveal how to brew it, and does not always work. Only the last two are fully true: the potency is not raised.
    • A failure costs only Bone Fragments. The potion is removed from the bag and added back right away, so you lose just the fee. A success also uses up the original potion (it is replaced by the blessed one).
    • The hidden formula counter. Each potion id has its own counter in your save (bless_times).
    • A 5% jackpot. Every attempt also makes a second roll from 1 to 100; a result below 6 (5%) counts as a jackpot.
    • The two rolls are probably the same dice (inferred). Both rolls are made with the same seed value, derived from the clock and the potion's position in the batch, and a roll with the same seed gives the same underlying random number.

    • Without the jackpot (pure successes needed) it would take 50 potions at 40%. Since you get the recipe as soon as the counter hits 20, the cheap potions are the cheapest way to learn their own formula.
    • Formulas only matter for some potions. Elixir of Health's formula is already covered by the early alchemy quest (15032), so the counter never gives anything.
    • The rolls follow the clock. The seed uses the last six digits of the system time (reversed) plus the potion's number in the batch, so there is no luck carried over between attempts, and no saved random state to exploit.
    • Blessed potions are not ingredients. The recipes for Elixir of Energy and others ask for the plain Elixir of Stamina and so on (id comparison), so blessing a potion that you need as an ingredient removes it from the supply (inferred).
    • Maze copies. Deep into the Dark Mist hands out ready-made blessed Elixirs of Inertia, Antitoxin, Energy, Recovery, Epiphany and Petrification Powder, plus a plain Elixir of Hallucination (item ids 72113 to 72119, see Consumables); they are not made by this service and do not touch the formula counters.

    Dedication#

    • One level for everybody. The code runs the same experience through every hero in your save, including heroes you do not play. A level-up always adds one attribute point to each of them, so team choice never changes your level.
    • Jerold follows Terrence. When a save is loaded without a stored Jerold entry, his level and experience are copied from Terrence's (fs_herolist.lua:84-95), so the shared level also covers the DLC hero.
    • Excess carries over. Offering one Filthy Core (7,500) at level 2 can cross several levels in one go. The leftover stays on the bar.
    • Waste only at the very top. At level 98 the game limits the offer to the number of bones that finish level 99 (it rounds up, so one bone may overshoot slightly). At lower levels the six-item ticks are not limited.
    • Only the shown hero is checked for the cap. The "level 99" refusal and the near-cap limit read the level of the hero on screen; since all heroes share the level this is the same number.
    • Bone Fragments are also your money. Every shop price is paid in the same item (see Bone Fragment and Merchants), so what you spend is what you do not dedicate.
    • Potion of Wealth doubles bone pick-ups for 600 s (Consumables), which stacks nicely with a farming trip before a big Dedication session.
    • The altar also reacts. The animation plays only if the altar is open (lit) and only when a single bone is offered, so the single-bone ticks of a hold animate and the multi-bone ticks (after 3 s) do not.
    • The tutorial order. The Dedicate guide (quest 15034) is shown before the Alchemy guide (quest 15035); see Alchemy.

    Carriage supplies#

    • Prices follow the loop, stock never runs out. The twelve basic rows have stock -1 and prices of roughly x1.5 / x2.2 / x3.4 across loops 2-4. Active Sendril, for instance, costs 3 / 5 / 8 / 12 Bone Fragments.
    • Each plant waits on a boss. Adamina Herbs and the Bone Whistle need the Adamina boss quest 21006; Distorting Vine and Trippy Flower need Exilium's quest 31002; Active Symbiote and Eroded Rock need Katib's 51001; Glass Ball and Edith Moss need Edith's 41001; Twilight Weed needs Blessedland's 61001.
    • The whale oils are only for DLC owners. Rows 99013-99016 have DLCSwitch 1, which the shop checks against the Deep into the Dark Mist DLC flag.
    • Whale oil gets dearer by tier but ignores loops. 5, 10 and 20 Big Bone for the three oils and 30 Bone Fragments for the lamp, the same in all four loops.

    Chapter merchants#

    • Only two items ever restock, and not everywhere. Elixir of Sanity (stalls 1040, 2009, 3003, 4096, 5117, 6093, 7056) and Elixir of Hallucination (3003, 4096, 5117, 6093, 7056) are the only rows flagged to refill, 12 rows in all, stock 5 each.
    • Formulas are bought once for the whole save. The 10 "Formula:" rows are flagged NotReset, so a formula you bought is never offered again, even after you start the next New Game Plus loop.
    • The "discount" line is flavour. The Katib, Karst Cave and Ichthyosauria sprites say they can give a discount, but they use the same price numbers as every other stall (for example Chipped Immundus is 20 / 30 / 45 / 65 at Edith, Katib, Karst Cave and Ichthyosauria alike).
    • Starry Honey Wine is sold in four of the seven story chapters. Among the chapter stalls it is only sold at Edith 4096, Katib 5117, Blessedland 6093 and Karst Cave 7056 (and at 9135 in the DLC), one bottle each and always for Big Bone (item description: it resets the character's attributes), so keep Big Bone back if you want a respec.
    • Throwables never run out. Throwing Knife and Huge Fang rows have stock -1 (unlimited) in every loop.
    • Heggem's Truth trophy levels up with the loop. Row 11001 sells a different trophy row in each loop (Truth +0, +1, +2, +3, ids 60025, 60225, 60325, 60425) at 100 / 120 / 140 / 180 Bone Fragments, one copy per loop.
    • Non-Existent Boots cost almost a thousand. Exilium row 31001 sells the boots trophy at 875 / 900 / 950 / 999 Bone Fragments, the dearest chapter-shop item by far, and the price barely rises with loops.
    • Concentration Rune uses the rarest coin. Karst Cave row 71007 asks 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 Filthy Cores. Filthy Core is the top dedication currency (see Currencies), and the stall is the only place that trades it for goods.

    Merchants#

    • No discounts exist. The Katib, Karst Cave and Ichthyosauria sprites claim they can offer "a good discount", but the prices are plain table numbers.
    • A stall can run out on the first visit. Immundus is limited per row (1 to 5 per stall).
    • Dying and resting are free restocks. Because the respawn routine refills elixirs, you can empty a stall of Sanity and Hallucination elixirs, rest, and buy 5 more.
    • Formula rows are forever. Once bought, a formula (such as Powder of Defence or Wildfire) is never offered again, even in later loops. The same goes for Viola's six trophies.
    • Contracts move Norwood and Viola's shelves. Three Broken Slates and three Ancient Relics in the story unlock new rows. Those quests are permanent, so Norwood's tier carries over into New Game Plus.
    • The whale oil is DLC-only and boss-gated. See Carriage supplies.
    • Stall copies don't share stock. Ichthyosauria's three identical stalls (9136-9138) each have their own counters. The two Heggem stalls and the pairs of stalls in the other chapters are different lists.
    • A gap in the Ichthyosauria rows. Row id 93008 does not exist in the data (ids run 93001 to 93011 with that one gap), so the DLC stall 9135 has ten rows, not eleven.

    Norwood's sundries#

    • Level = tier + loop. The trophy level sold is the tier number plus min(loop, 3), so the highest possible is level 6 (+5). Loop 4 sells the same levels as loop 3 but charges more.
    • The Contract progress is permanent. The quests 75051-75053 have NotReset 1, so Norwood stays at his highest tier when you start a new loop; it is the loop price column that moves instead.
    • A new tier removes the old one. Each row has both a "show after" and a "hide after" quest, so doing a Contract does not add rows, it swaps the whole block.
    • The only route to +5. Only tier 3 in loop 3 or 4 sells the maximum level (+5, trophy level 6), for 111 or 150 Anthozoan Bones each.

    Safe house shop#

    • Big Bone is cheap here. Item 40002 costs only 2 Spectre Mushrooms. Big Bone is the currency for the better chapter-stall goods, and the ordinary Black Sprite and Carriage shops buy it back for 7 Bone Fragments.
    • Only one Dream Chest is on sale at a time. The chest tier advances as each of the four maze bosses falls (quests 4991001 to 4991004). Dream Chest odds are in Dream Chest.
    • Selling is capped by the bag. Spectre Mushrooms stack to 9,999 and the shop refuses a trophy sale whose payout would push you past that.
    • Offering is separate from shopping. Tutorial texts 1072-1075 say that offering Spectre Mushrooms makes the Marred in the Dark Mist stronger; mushrooms spent at this shop are only a currency.

    Viola's collection#

    • Prices are fixed. Brass Ring of Handel costs 4 and Figurine of Sorrowful Nun 5 from the start; Contract 1 adds Shimmering Shard (6) and The Rose (10), Contract 2 Fanatical Sand (15) and Contract 3 Brooch with Sacred Pattern (20): 60 Dominator Crystals for the whole set.
    • One purchase per save. Because NotReset is 1, a trophy bought in loop 1 stays sold out in loop 2. If you want a second copy you cannot buy it.
    • The consumable block is a New Game Plus gift. In loop 1 the four rows have item 0 and price 0, so they are skipped (GetOnSellItemVirtualList ignores rows with item id 0).
    • No returns. Viola's own six trophies have no sell price (Price -1), so the sell tab will not take them back.

    Bosses#

    • The phase change is player-driven. A boss does not change at a health threshold; it changes when your sanity falls, which is why a bigger SanDamage means an earlier phase 2 and why Consumables that restore sanity can hold a boss in phase 1.
    • The 50% heal can undo your work. If you were above 50% of the boss's health when you turned Lunatic, nothing is lost; if you were below, the boss refills to half.
    • Not every boss counts sanity. Terror Obsession counts hammer charges, the First Messenger counts health bars, Proxy Higgins uses summons for its second stage, and the Maddened Ascetic rematch starts directly in phase 2.
    • Several bosses have a second life through a weak point or a hidden counter: Edith needs both hands destroyed before she can be executed (x36 execution damage), Fused Sprenger has a glowing weak point with its own hidden ...
    • 2 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Bosses.

    Edith#

    • Hand damage costs you no sanity. The passthrough subtracts HP from Edith directly, not through the normal hit path that charges sanity, and the hand's own SanDamage is 0.
    • A forced collapse does not need Resilience damage. Her Resilience is not drained by your hits; it is set to 0 by the hand check.
    • The x36 bonus lasts 120 s but the collapse is short. The status duration is 120 s; it is removed when she gets up (ClearStatusById(10075)).
    • Lock-on is off for most of the fight, which is why the camera does not snap to her body.
    • 3 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Edith.

    Elmar (the Butcher)#

    • Hitting her costs sanity, but little. Each hit takes SanDamage x HP removed / her max HP from your sanity; her SanDamage is only 20, so killing her from full HP costs 20 sanity.
    • She never changes phase, even if you are Lunatic. The fight controller only starts the boss phase change (slow motion, 2 s black fade) when the boss's grade equals its "grade threshold".
    • She is not "boss-flagged" in the model data. The model row (ES_HeroData 20012) has Boss 0, while the placement in ES_StoryMonster has IsBoss 1.
    • The Teresa summon is a different case. In the Chapter 8 boss fight the same model is summoned without a body collider or lock-on, so it behaves like a roaming hazard rather than a duel opponent (inferred from the call-condition code).

    Frollo#

    • Blocking does not stop stun build-up. Every stun row has GuardEnable 0, so a blocked bell hit still adds to the stun gauge (see Stun).
    • He has an execution and a backstab in his kit. His AI switches to an "Execute" state when the target is in a Weakened hit reaction, and the Execute behaviour can use his execute or backstab move.
    • Hits on him cost 55 sanity per full HP. That means Abnormal after 55% of his HP and Lunatic only after 91% of it. Being Lunatic changes nothing in his behaviour.
    • His second stat row is a duplicate. ES_StoryMonsterProperty lists two grades, but all the values are the same, so even if he switched grade nothing would change.

    Fused Sprenger#

    • The weak point is a separate, hidden health bar. 4% of his maximum HP, refilled after 20 s. It only counts damage taken through the weak collider.
    • In a normal story run the weak point only exists after you have the Sand Glass. His script switches the glowing weak-point equipment and its collider off unless quest 52201 is complete (data comment: obtaining the sand glass ...
    • Breaking it switches off one of his best attacks. The teleport branch of the AI is skipped while the weak point is broken (inferred from GetWeakEnable() in attack20034.lua).
    • The teleport cannot be interrupted. During the vanish his Poise and Resilience are locked at 1, so there is nothing to break.
    • 4 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Fused Sprenger.

    Holy Father#

    • There are only two enchants. The check hpPercent > 0.2 stops the third step (10%).
    • Enchanted moves speed up your path to Lunatic. Besides the sanity you lose for damaging him (85 sanity per full HP, so Lunatic after about 59% of his HP, and Abnormal after 35%), each enchanted hit that lands costs a further 2 to 5 sanity.
    • Phase 2 starts with a heal. If he is below 50% when you go Lunatic, he is raised to exactly 50%.
    • Phase 2 defence jump. At Loop 1 a hit with an attack value of 300 meets 79 defence in phase 1 (ratio 3.8, factor 0.49, about 146 damage) and 159 defence in phase 2 (ratio 1.9, factor 0.36, about 108 damage), 26% less (using the factor table of Damage formula).
    • 2 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Holy Father.

    Jenny of Exilium#

    • Phase 2 comes from your sanity, not her HP. With SanDamage 100, you lose exactly as much sanity as the percentage of her HP you remove.
    • While the leap chains run she cannot be staggered. Her script sets the Poise and Resilience floors to 1 for the whole of O, NOP, NO and GiantHand (and for the air drop and air shot), so a hit cannot empty them.
    • She passes through everything during chains. The same moves switch on "move through all" and flying, and the model is hidden at some jump points (for example the final leg of NOP and NO, and the laser), so she can vanish between leaps.
    • After the giant hand she is placed on a fixed arena point. GiantHand ends by setting her position to scene marker 11 (inferred: the arena's centre or her home position).
    • 2 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Jenny of Exilium.

    Maddened Ascetic#

    • Phase 2 is controlled by your sanity, not his HP. The fight controller checks on every update whether he is still in phase 1 and the player is Lunatic.
    • He heals to 50% on the change. If you push him to Lunatic while his HP is under 50%, he is topped up to exactly 50% of his maximum. Above 50% nothing is added.
    • A phase-1 kill is remembered. Killing a boss that never reached phase 2 sets a "killed a boss while not Lunatic" flag for the whole run; if that flag stays unset until the end of the game, an achievement for killing every boss in the Lunatic state is granted.
    • The phase 2 kill unlocks a line later. Quest 91101 is a permanent flag.
    • 2 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Maddened Ascetic.

    Rosen Cavalier Patrick#

    • He barely costs sanity. SanDamage is 5, so killing him from full HP costs 5 sanity, compared with 90 to 120 for the main bosses.
    • His guard and dodge are real combat actions, not scripts. The AI presses the same buttons a hero would (the Special button for the guard, the counter-attack slot for the parry, the dodge slot for the dodges), so the normal hit-reaction rules apply (inferred from the AI file).
    • Counter priority decides who wins the clash. The AI checks whether your current move is "counter-attackable" and whether his guard move has a higher counter priority than your move (hitSkillData.CounterAttack > attSkillData.CounterAttack).
    • No level scaling for Poise. His Poise (10) is the same at all four loops, but his HP is 3.8 times higher and his attack 3.5 times in Loop 4+.

    Terror Obsession#

    • The counter is the real boss. The phase is decided by the charge counter, not by sanity or HP: that is why the report shows the change after four charges.
    • The instant-kill circle ignores everything. It sets HP to 0 through its own script, bypassing defence and resistances.
    • No loop scaling in the story. His story monster row has level 1 in all four columns, so his normal hits are the same on every loop.
    • No sanity at all. Neither hits to him nor his kill change your sanity (SanDamage 0), so he cannot push you into the Lunatic state.
    • 4 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Terror Obsession.

    Elena (first duel)#

    • She counters on a timer and on your attacks. Her counter (S1, mirrored by NS1) is a guard that turns into a riposte.
    • In this duel she cannot dodge. Her dodge branch only runs while her skill condition is 2, the two-sword stance (attack20039.lua:394, and the dodge row ES_SkillData HeroId 20039 skill 102 has SkillCondition 2).
    • She retreats with a random back or side jump. DP has a 30% chance (25% in the second stance) and a 7 s cooldown, and moves her about 3.6 to 3.7 m sideways or backwards.
    • No sanity phase, but your sanity still drops. Each point of her health you remove costs you 55 / max health sanity, so dealing 100% of her health costs 55 sanity.
    • 1 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Elena (first duel).

    Elena (second duel)#

    • Counter in one-sword stance, dodge in two-sword stance. She counters (S1/NS1, SkillCondition 1) when she has been stuck in hit reaction for more than 2 s or while you start an attack close to her.
    • Stance memory. The skill condition is stored on her, not on a timer: she stays in two swords until LP2 completes.
    • Sanity cost 55. Removing all her health costs 55 sanity, so a full-sanity hero ends the fight near the Lunatic line but she never changes (see Lunatic state).
    • Loot scaling. The Fractured Body count rises with the New Game Plus loop (3, 6, 9, 13), while the Blackwater Crystal stays at 1.

    Guderian the Guardian#

    • Crit bonus is cut to 70%. His Crit-resist stat of 700 multiplies the attacker's critical damage bonus by 0.7 (animalbase_value.lua:2463-2467).
    • Stances are independent of sanity. Going Lunatic does not change his moves; only the AI's own switches do.
    • Sanity cost 130. The Lunatic line is reached after about 38% of his Health (inferred from SanDamage x damage / max Health), but nothing changes for the boss.
    • Grapple pull. The grappling claw DP2 (7 to 13 m, 30%, 8 s cooldown) hooks and pulls you in from 13 m, so staying at range is not a free dodge in the scythe stance.
    • 1 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Guderian the Guardian.

    Maddened Ascetic (rematch)#

    • A free second phase. On a normal run the Ascetic only reaches phase 2 if you go Lunatic.
    • The loot table is a roll, not a chest. Because the Immundus Diamond sits in its own list with 100% probability, it is the only reward you can count on.
    • Sanity cost 75. Removing the whole Health bar costs 75 sanity, so you only reach the Lunatic line of 50 after about two thirds of its Health, and at that point nothing changes for the boss (it is already in phase 2).
    • Dark ultimate and the Special blast. The ultimate OP (160 dark, 180 degree cone, 6.5 m, guard break 150) and the Special S (119 dark, -15 sanity, 120 degree cone) are its two dark-damage moves; the Special also removes sanity.

    Proxy Higgins#

    • Two move sets inside stage 2. Every skill row has a style condition.
    • Stage 3 locks his Health at 1. On entering stage 3 the code sets a minimum of 1 on Health and -1 on Poise and Resilience (hero20040.lua:198-203), so ordinary damage cannot kill him there; his row has FinalHit = 1, so the last blow is handed to a scripted scene.
    • Stage 1 is an overlay, not a fight. The +9999 values are applied to Poise, Resilience and Defence, not to his Health.
    • He heals, and the heal has a threshold. NLP ("waters the flowers") is only chosen at 40% Health or lower (SkillNLPHpRate = 0.4), with a 35% chance and a 35 s cooldown.
    • 4 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Proxy Higgins.

    The First Fallen: Handel#

    • His Health is clamped in phase 2. When he is promoted, the game sets a minimum Health of max x 0.15 - 1 (SkillNO2HpPer = 0.15, hero20053.lua:74-85, hero20053_define.lua:56), so no hit can take him below about 15%.
    • He ends the fight himself. Once he is rampaging and at 1.5% Health or lower (SkillOOHpPer = 0.015 in the AI, attack20053.lua:246), he performs the four-part charged slash OO.
    • He cannot be staggered during his big set pieces. While the chant OP, the berserk start NO2, the charged slash OO and the berserk ender NLP start, the code sets a minimum of 1 on Poise and Resilience ...
    • Phase 1 has no clamp. If you never go Lunatic, he can be killed normally in phase 1.
    • 2 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of The First Fallen: Handel.

    The First Messenger#

    • Stage 2 can be a multi-part Health lock (inferred). The lock is only applied while IsCallHeroSanEnable is true, which means the list of Nightmare doubles to summon is not yet used up (hero10006.lua:84-90); with an empty list the lock is removed (SetPropertyLockMin(Hp, -1)).
    • Each stage change restores her Health. Whatever is left of a bar is lost.
    • Her sanity phase does not exist. Her grade threshold of 4 means the sanity-based promotion test can never pass for her (hero10006.lua:218, controllerstorymode.lua:2259).
    • The real body. In stage 3 the clones copy her dashes and drags.
    • 2 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of The First Messenger.

    Treasure Thieves#

    • No weak points, no Resilience shortcut. Every weak-point multiplier in his model is 0, so hits to any body part do the same damage, and breaking his armour only gives the guaranteed critical hit.
    • Crit bonus cut to 60%. His crit-resist stat of 600 multiplies your crit damage bonus by 0.6.
    • The parry heals him. DP4 is only chosen at 70% Health or lower (SkillDP4HPPercent = 0.7), has a 30% chance and a 15 s cooldown; the second part restores 5% of his maximum Health (hero20055.lua:728-740, HpAddPercent = 0.05).
    • He can only dodge in state 2. Every skill row of this model carries a state number in SkillCondition (state 1 rows 1-3 and 20-24, state 2 rows 4-10 and 102, state 3 rows 11-14, state 4 rows 15-19), and the Dodge row (102) ...

    Usurper#

    • Phase 2 is a stat change as well as a move change. The extra 200 Defence roughly doubles his physical toughness at loop 1 (178 becomes 378) and adds 78% at loop 4 (258 to 458).
    • The Health refill. Going Lunatic tops him up to 50%. If he is above 50% when you reach the fade nothing changes; below 50% he regains the difference.
    • Sanity cost 130. About 38% of his Health (50 / 130) takes you from full sanity to the Lunatic line, so phase 2 normally starts after roughly a third of the fight (inferred from SanDamage x damage / max Health).
    • Unblockable means dodge. In phase 2 the falling lightning (NS2), thunder pillars (NSP2), five orbs (NO2), jumping cut (O2), accelerated cut (OP2), lightning (NOP2) and shockwave (NLP2) are all unblockable ...

    World, modes and progression#

    Maps, quests, NPCs, lore systems, game modes and unlocks.

    Death and respawn#

    • The bone penalty is random between 25 and 35 percent and applies only after the last hero falls.
    • Alchemy trophies of the "destroyed on death" kind disappear only on a team wipe, not when one hero falls.
    • Ground items vanish on respawn; only the Heggie stash and scene markers stay.
    • The swap-in after a death keeps the boss-fight sanity percentage, which makes a swap a poor sanity reset.
    • Rested monsters return on death, but a defeated boss is tied to a quest flag.

    Drops and loot#

    • "Nothing" is a real weight, and full flasks remove the potion row from the total: with full flasks the potion groups simply never pay out.
    • The Lucky Madness label (+50 %) does not match the data (+5 or +10 weight points).
    • A first kill replaces rather than adds to the drop list, and the quest flag can be shared between several enemies.
    • A sanity drop is a second chance: it requires only a non-normal sanity state at the moment of the kill and goes straight to the bag, so it cannot be missed on the ground.
    • The maze table scales by maze level, the story table by New Game Plus loop.

    Heggie offerings#

    • Every Heggie contains exactly one correct offering, and the game never says which. This table is the answer key.
    • A satisfied Heggie is permanent; an offended one only leaves until the next death.
    • The sanity option is a hidden bonus sink: it is blocked below 50 % sanity and strict about the amount.
    • Reward items depend only on the completed flag, so a saved finished Heggie never gives the reward twice.

    Traps and tricks#

    • A trap's "Damage" column powers its status; it is not HP damage.
    • Walk-only zones also shrink your dodge roll to 70 % (boss corrosion) or 40 % (mud).
    • A trap affects every character whose team differs from the team that created it, so the lure and the stun smoke bomb hit the other side (stun smoke: 5.5 radius for 10 s).
    • Maze depth silently raises the damage of every trick and trap in steps at levels 8, 15 and 22.
    • The environmental spikes and boulders ignore team and hit for 999 toughness, so they always stagger heroes and monsters alike.

    World mechanics#

    • Flags decide everything. If a door, wall or pickup is missing, check its Show and Hide flags first: a single stray Hide flag removes it permanently.
    • Hide beats show, and Show needs every flag.
    • Locked-door hints can unlock things because they may complete a flag.
    • Maze depth silently scales hazards at levels 8, 15 and 22.
    • Loop-based scene pickups: the amount of a pickup differs between loops in 170 of 1212 rows (usually more in later loops, though a few rows give fewer) and 58 pickups change identity.
    • Virtual walls have almost no data (eight rows); most of their behavior lives in the scene.
    • Mud, the Slow status (10076) and some story rules force walking with a 0.4 dodge-speed factor.

    Carriage#

    • The Carriage is combat-free by design. While story id is 99 the code removes attacks (CheckSkillCommandEnable returns false), lock-on, execution and backstab, and enemy AI is never created.
    • All heroes are loaded. On entering the Carriage the game loads every hero you have unlocked, plus all their skins, so you can see them on the hero select panel and in the outfit menu.
    • You are always a fresh arrival. Going to the Carriage resets your position and respawn point to the Carriage, clears the kill list of the region you left (see Maps), and sets the "called from" story so that the Carriage knows which region to show first.
    • Carriage arrival scenes. Each region has a small table of "carriage points" describing how Glenn arrives or leaves. The "Transfer" flag marks stops that skip the arrival ride and just place you.
    • Katib's third point (an area-2 transfer) and the Karst Cave's third and fourth points are hidden return points used by scripts (the third brings you back to the Karst Cave prison after Viola's dream execution, quest 73711).
    • After the ending you cannot enter the Colossal Grave again, according to the game text. A New Journey is always started from Glenn in the Carriage.
    • Residents move on. Because residents are tied to quests, the Carriage looks different at each stage: Viola alone at first, then Viola and Norwood together, Benita after Exilium, and Viola and Norwood disappearing after the Usurper cutscene.

    Chapters#

    • The chapter order the data gives is not the order you play. Chapter ids 4 and 5 are swapped relative to the journal (see above).
    • Each chapter's boss quest unlocks vendor stock. The vendor in the Carriage sells a new plant per boss, so skipping a boss (when you can) delays alchemy supplies. See Carriage supplies and Alchemy.
    • Companions leave and come back by "revert" quests. The game removes heroes with "disable hero" quest actions, and later quests list the earlier ones in a revert field; completing the later quest undoes the earlier one and re-enables the hero.
    • Jerold is gated. Quest actions that enable Jerold only work if the DLC join quest has been completed.
    • Returning is a quest, too. The chapters are chained entirely by quest flags, such as "boss dead", "cutscene done" and "dialog choice", which is why the same bells open different stops in a New Game Plus run.

    • Heggie count. The frog table has 40 rows (5 per chapter for chapters 1 to 7 and the DLC), but the Naturalist achievement needs only 35, so five can be missed.

    • Third carriage point has the transfer flag. Point 3 (area 2) is marked as a transfer point (what the flag does is not documented; it is the same flag all four Karst Cave points carry).

    Deep into the Dark Mist#

    • The lamp is a key, not a light. Its tier only decides which of the four areas the keeper lets you enter.
    • Difficulty is paid for twice. Raising levelAdd costs 25 to 100 mushrooms, but raises every drop table, so higher levels pay back (boss mushrooms rise by 2 per maze level, see Maze rewards).
    • The first Border run skips the offering. Every other area asks the question from its first visit.
    • Shrines never repeat identically. The pity counter guarantees a statue or crate at least every second arena and a fountain at least every third.
    • Bosses do not repeat inside a run. The kill list is stored per run; the same boss is not drawn again until every boss in a floor's pool is dead.
    • Enemy levels follow a fixed curve. Almost every enemy has level 3 x maze level + 6 from maze level 5 on, so by the end of the Cave (level 23) they are level 75; at 27 they are level 87. See Maze rewards.
    • Death is a full reset of buffs. The hub entry code clears every status, including the extra-life stacks bought from statues.
    • HP and sanity persist between arenas but statues' buffs last only 300 seconds.
    • The maze needs the add-on. Entering without it shows the purchase message (UI_COMMON_ACTION_MAZE_WALL); Arcade mode also checks for it.

    Maze rewards#

    • The number of mushrooms per kill is tiny; bosses and crates are the bank. A level 1 kill gives about 1.25 mushrooms, an elite 6 and a Nightmare crate 44-50, a boss 41-50.
    • Potions refill themselves. The Health Potion roll disappears when your potion stock is full, so full-stock hero kills do not waste a roll on potions.
    • Item chance triples over the run. The item share goes 5% to 10% to 15% per roll at maze levels 1, 5 and 16.
    • Guaranteed trophy. Every boss kill drops exactly one trophy (no "nothing" row), and its tier is fixed by the maze level, not luck.
    • Low-level trophies are +0. Maze level 1 is always +0.
    • Trophy strength is a second roll. Within a tier, the small, medium and large versions of a trophy have equal weight, so each drop has a one in three chance of each strength (except Edith's and Frollo's trophies, which have one strength).
    • Traps scale in four steps (maze levels 8, 15, 22).
    • Mushrooms are not a score. Nothing in the code converts leftover mushrooms to anything; a won run just leaves you with the coins.

    Maze statues#

    • Two saints, even odds, but buffs are not even. Offer 2 appears about 45% of the time per saint, the last offer about 29%.
    • Sanity trades are half price and half reward. The percentages in the descriptions are twice the real values.
    • Stamina recovery buff is the biggest percentage (+35%), attack is the smallest (+17.5%).
    • Mushroom doubling covers every pickup that uses auto-pickup, probably including the 41 to 100 mushrooms a boss drops (see Maze rewards); it is the strongest offer when taken before a boss or a Nightmare crate.
    • Potions are convertible. A Health Potion costs 5 mushrooms and an offering of 250 Health buys 20 mushrooms; mushrooms are the maze's money, see Deep into the Dark Mist.

    Game modes#

    • One number picks the mode. FS_SaveStory.StoryMode has seven values (Normal 1, BossChallenge 2, JumpGame 3, Travel 4, Maze 5, BossTimeChallenge 6, Arcade 7), and dozens of checks like "is this a boss time challenge?" change behaviour: for example two boss counter-attack rules only ...
    • Only the story reads the journey. GetLogicLoop returns 1 in Arcade regardless of the counter, and the maze and the challenge ignore the column completely.
    • Add-ons are checked by flags. The lobby tests IapData flags: the Arcade button needs the Arcade add-on, a maze-only challenge boss needs the maze add-on, and some Arcade stages need the maze or Tides content (the game says "Add-on content Deep into the Dark Mist is required").
    • A mode can borrow your save or ignore it. The Obsession Challenge (custom) copies the hero from your save but writes its results to its own record; Arcade uses level 30 presets and never touches your trophies; the maze uses a separate hero list.
    • Journeys are a story-only loop. The endless counter only advances when you accept a New Journey in the story (Arcade's own restart also adds 1 but uses the journey 1 column).

    New Game Plus enemy scaling#

    • There is no enemy multiplier, only a level column. Everything scales through the level, so enemy defence and resistances rise with it as well as HP and attack.
    • Trap damage by Dark Mist floor, not by journey. In the Dark Mist maze (Deep into the Dark Mist) traps and tricks use the journey columns but pick the column from the maze floor level: floors 1 to 7 use column 1, floors 8 to 14 column 2, floors 15 to 21 column 3, and floor 22 and above column 4.
    • Dark Mist side areas follow your hero. In the Dark Mist side areas (stories 101 to 105, scenes 20001 to 29999, entered through hidden wall passages and draining sanity while you are inside) the enemy level is the row's level plus your hero's level plus 10.
    • Two bosses riposte sooner in journey 2 and later. The game suppresses the counter-attack of Rosen Cavalier Patrick (while in his defence stance) and the Usurper (special stance) until the boss has blocked 1 (Patrick) or 2 (Usurper) of your hits.
    • Maze, Obsession Challenge and Dance of the Throne ignore the journey. Arcade forces the data column to journey 1 and uses its own monster property table; the maze uses the maze floor; challenge bosses use their challenge level.
    • Levels never adapt to you. The only places where your hero's level feeds into an enemy's level are the Dark Mist side areas described above. Everywhere else the number is fixed in the table.

    New Game Plus loot and shops#

    • A "nothing" row is part of the roll. In the common drop tables the odds of getting nothing really are 24 to 25% on average and are never zero, and they shrink in the late journeys of tables that carry the Blackwater items (Exilium 30006 goes from 30 to 1).
    • The save decides. The tables are swapped whenever a journey starts or a save is loaded (SetLoop), so loading any save applies that save's journey. Journey 5 and above are the same as journey 4, including prices.
    • Big Bone takes over from journey 2. The guaranteed beast tables 10015 (Heggem and Adamina) pay 8-10 or 10-12 Bone Fragments in journey 1 but only 3-4, 5 and 5-6 Big Bone in journeys 2 to 4 (4 in Adamina's journey 2), and one of the two rows of table 10014 swaps to Big Bone too.
    • Prices rise, but the safe house does not. Safe-house and Viola prices do not scale; chapter stalls and the Carriage do.
    • Instant-kill damage never scales. Three tricks stay at 99,999 damage in every journey (see New Game Plus enemy scaling).

    New Game Plus#

    • The journey number is capped in the data, not in the save. The save keeps counting journeys without any limit (the "is loop max" check always returns false), but every table read uses min(journey, 4).
    • Legendary Courier has a loophole. Legendary Courier needs "journey 4 or more without using the Egg of the Bearer".
    • Level is not your level. Enemies come in at the level in the table, however strong your carried-over hero is. In journey 2 an enemy in Heggem is level 25 to 30 whether you are level 5 or level 70.
    • Two boss counters wake up. In journey 1 the Rosen Cavalier Patrick and the Usurper do not counter-attack during their defence stance until they have blocked 1 and 2 hits.
    • Flags decide what is "new". Quest 99999 ("journey 2 or later") is a NotReset flag completed at the start of a restart. Content that appears or disappears in later journeys is gated on it, not on the loop counter.
    • Maze and Arcade ignore all of this. The Dark Mist maze uses its own floor levels, and Arcade forces the journey column to 1 (its restart still adds 1 to the counter, but the "logic loop" is 1).
    • Loot does not need you to wait for journey 4. Region progress already raises trophy levels in the first journey (+0 to +2); the journeys then push the same drop to +3.
    • Killing bosses again. Boss-defeat quests are not NotReset, so they clear on restart: the Carriage plant shop rows that depended on them lock again until you kill the bosses once more (Carriage supplies).

    Arcade mode#

    • Elena's talents differ between the two ways to play. Stage select hands her all 31 talents of rows 1 to 3 at maximum (everything except the seven achievement talents), so she is far stronger there than in a full run, where the code gives her no preset talents at all (AddTalentToMaxWithOutSan is called only from ArcadeStageSelect).
    • Only Elena's full clear counts for unlocks. Expert and stage select check hero 10007 specifically.
    • Expert is easier to S than Normal on all seven chapter stages (S thresholds are lower), although its enemies are 5 levels higher.
    • Elena's stamina-cost talents probably do nothing. Fleeting, Eternal War and Demiurge's Wish (17403 to 17405) are type 103 with the condition string "1".
    • The Health and Stamina talents have misleading labels. Path of Suffering is described as a greater Health bonus than Shadow Moon, yet both are +30%.
    • The combo-stamina talent only works after the third consecutive attack (TalentComboAttackNum is 3), like Jerold's.
    • Score cap and the damage cap are anti-cheat. A stage score of 32,000 or more, or a physical damage stat above 242, is silently not recorded.
    • Rank multiplier counts monster deaths, not points. Killing the many weak enemies matters for x1.2 as much as killing a boss, and respawning enemies are excluded so they cannot be farmed.
    • Casual is a damage cut, not an enemy cut. Enemies are the same as on Normal; you simply take 35% less damage, have 130 more Toughness and 20% more base Stamina.
    • Altars in the mode. The altar text for the mode says resting restores health and Health Potions "or obtains new Talents". Only the Nightmare kills were found to grant talents in code.

    Dance of the Throne#

    • Name mismatch. Players and the menu say "Dance of the Throne"; the code, data tables and debug flags say "Arcade".
    • Two Elenas share one Chinese name. The playable hero (10007) and the duelling nun boss of Blessedland (20039) both carry the internal name 伊琳娜 (Elena).
    • Only Elena's clears unlock. Expert and stage select in Arcade mode need a full clear by Elena herself, see Arcade mode.
    • Elena's stamina-cost talents from this add-on probably have no effect because of a condition quirk, see Arcade mode. That is a reading of the code and has not been verified in play.

    Obsession Challenge#

    • The sub-mode names are swapped between code and menu. In code "Normal" is the mode with your own save and "Custom" is the preset mode, the opposite of what you might guess. The achievement texts use the menu names.
    • Colossus difficulty is boss level 89. Levels 20, 40 and 60 for the lower tiers; the jump to 89 plus the weakness bonuses and large HP additions makes level 4 far harder than level 3 for most bosses.
    • Phase 1 is almost untouched. For bosses with two phases (Ascetic, Holy Father, Jenny, Fused Sprenger, Edith, Usurper, Handel), the HP, resistance and damage bonuses sit on the phase 2 row.
    • Standard Mode bosses lose HP. At level 5 many bosses have negative HP bonuses (Terror Obsession -1,200, Guderian -2,500, Treasure Thieves -4,000, Handel's phase 2 -4,400, Edith's phase 2 -5,000), and a level-15 base.
    • The Treasure Thieves get weaker as you raise the level. Their HP bonus is 0 / -1,000 / -2,000 / -4,500 at levels 1 to 4 (while their resistance rises 40 / 60 / 80 / 100). This looks like a tuning quirk of the data.
    • Time limit is score-only. There is no fail timer in the code searched; it only sets the slope of the score line.
    • Teresa's special cases. She is the longest fight (960 s limit), is the only boss whose row uses story id 8 and boss id 8001 (the main-story fight), and in Standard Mode you are handed 10 Throwing Knives.
    • Jerold needs the Tides DLC flag even though the button exists for everyone: clicking Jerold's portrait does nothing when IapData.GetEnterTide() is false, and the portrait is greyed.
    • **Fights use the challenge StoryId/Area.** The Patrick, Frollo and Elena challenge fights run in story 699 areas 1, 2 and 3, not in their chapter maps.

    Tides of Oblivion#

    • Where the game checks the Tide flag (IapData.GetEnterTide): the map destination list, the dialogue filter (DLCShow), the lobby logo (only when the DLC_Elena constant is false, which it is not in this build, see Dance of the Throne), Obsession Challenge's Jerold button, Arcade stages 2002 to 2004, and the Dark Mist maze hero check.
    • One quest flag unlocks all three bosses. Quest 91209 is completed by Handel's death, and Guderian and the Treasure Thieves reuse it as their challenge unlock, so you can fight them in Obsession Challenge as soon as Handel falls even if you skipped them in the story (inferred from the shared flag).
    • Standard Mode strength. In the preset mode these three bosses lose a lot of HP (Guderian -2,500, Treasure Thieves -4,000, Handel's second phase -4,400), comparable to the biggest cuts of the base-game bosses (Edith's second phase loses 5,000).
    • Names with no English entry. Several expansion enemies appear in the data only with Chinese internal names, so they cannot be named reliably here; see Enemies.
    • Expansion naming. "Tide" is the code name; "dlcstory1" is the store id; the English title is confirmed from the text sections. Nothing in the files uses a different English name.

    Achievements#

    • Two layers of tracking. Each save keeps counters (achievementList) and an "already counted" id list per counter (achievementStatisticsList).
    • Counters survive new journeys. Starting a new journey (FS_SaveStory:Restart) does not clear the counters or the "already counted" lists. Everything with a Max above 1 keeps accumulating across journeys of the same save.
    • The achievement screen does not show progress. The progress text of every row is hard-set to "1"; the counters are never read by the screen.
    • Difficulty gate. RecordAchievement discards the event when the achievement's leastUnlockDiff is higher than the current difficulty.
    • Mode gate. In the Obsession Challenge (time-challenge mode) RecordAchievement always returns without doing anything, so no story achievement can be earned there. In Arcade mode it only accepts ids from 60000 to 70000.
    • Platinum rule. Pascal's Wager (12014) needs 42 finished achievements with id below 20000, which is every other achievement in the first tab. It is awarded by two redundant checks (per-save counter and global count).
    • Strong Obsession staging. "Defeat a Strong Obsession" and "Kill all Strong Obsessions" refer to final bosses whose grade was raised by Lunatic.
    • Challenge is checked at the end. Challenge (12013) is evaluated only at the end of a run: Terrence's level must still be 1.
    • Legendary Courier. At the end of a run the game requires journey number at least 4 (BattleData.GameLoopMax) and that the Egg of the Bearer (item 10018) was not used in that journey.
    • Ingenuity and Great Master. The two +5 trophy achievements are staged: each time a trophy reaches the maximum strength level (internal level 6) the game first tries to bump Great Master if Ingenuity is already complete, then adds one to Ingenuity.
    • Alchemist needs 17 of 18. The data lists 18 formula quests (Q12014 and Q22405 to Q22421) but the counter stops at 17, so one formula can be skipped.
    • Crafting counters count batch size. Druggist, Merchant in the War and Blacksmith add the quantity you craft in one confirmation, not one per craft.
    • Beginner and Grandmaster use the word "Immundus" for talent stones. Beginner triggers the first time any talent is raised. Grandmaster adds the number of Immundus Diamonds (item 50004) consumed.
    • Obsession Challenge naming. The constants behind 50002 and 50003 are called "killAllBossInCustomMode" and "killAllBossInNormalModeVeryHard".
    • Dance of the Throne checks. Return to Sender needs 10 parried projectiles inside a 10 second window (it compares each new parry to the one made 9 parries earlier).
    • Casual players. On Casual you can still earn everything that is not flagged above, including the base-game collection counters (Siflers, documents, Heggies), the crafting counters and the Ichthyosauria Sifler and Heggie ones.

    Collectibles#

    • Everything accumulates across journeys. A collectible is recorded by the quest id that fed it, and the record is stored in the save and is never cleared when a new journey starts (FS_SaveStory:Restart leaves the achievement counters and the lists of counted quest ids alone).
    • Heggie progress is not permanent. The satisfaction value is stored in a temporary table that the game rebuilds when the area is refreshed (the same routine that respawns monsters and restores health), so partial progress is lost when the area resets (inferred from the routine that clears the table).
    • The three offers are fixed per Heggie. The order of rewards (+1, 0, -1) is stored in the data, not randomized, so a Heggie always asks for the same thing.
    • Alchemist has a spare formula. One of the 18 formula quests is not needed, see above.
    • Anthozoan Bones counts every acquisition. Illusion (1000) adds the quantity each time the item is put into the bag, from any source, and spending bones on Heggies or other uses does not reduce it.
    • The bottles. The Dazzling Melos Bottle (trophy 60075) is handed out by quest 55069 and the Imperishable Melos Bottle (trophy 60080) by quest 55087, both in chapter 5.
    • Lost Valleys are chapter-bound. The five valleys are stories 101 to 105, entered from chapters 1 to 5. The achievement stores the valley id, so each one counts only once.
    • DLC counters are difficulty gated. Pieces of Old Days (28 Ichthyosauria documents) is flagged for Normal difficulty and is dropped on Casual. Trading Without Borders (4 Siflers) and Dominator's Revelry (5 Heggies) are not.

    Skins#

    • Model id arithmetic. A hero's outfit is a different model whose id is built from the base hero: 10000 + base index + skin index x 1000. Terrence's outfit is 11001, Viola's 11002, Benita's 11003 and Elena's 11007.
    • Where the outfit really appears. UpdateSkin is only run in the story battle type, so the outfit is a model swap during the story.
    • Enemy Elena has her own look swap. In Arcade mode the code turns enemy model 20039 (Elena as a boss, stage 1006) into model 21039 whenever the player's Elena is wearing the default outfit.
    • Flag always on in this build. In this Lua build the add-on records are created with valid = true, and the only assignments to valid anywhere in the SDK helper set it to true as well.
    • No unlock path for Norwood and Jerold. Both have only their default look: GetSkinMax is 0 for them, so the outfit button never appears.

    Church of the Blessedland#

    • Witch's Notes are contraband. Witch's Notes, written in the Blessedland's language, are common there but strictly forbidden elsewhere by the Church (ItemData 10003), which is why finding one in Heggem is a clue.
    • The Hound Amulet is a Church military item. It was a noble hunting charm, but the Church's expansion pushed it into military use (ItemData 20031).
    • Spectre Mushrooms were a Church tool. The Church used them to attract Marred and clear land around the Blessedland (ItemData 40031), the same mushrooms players offer in the Mist Camp (see The Dark Mist and Sendrils).
    • Church keys leave with the Journey. The Elevator, Chapel, Tower, Trinity Hall, Revival Altar and Confessional keys all have GamePlusRemove = 1 (ItemData 10028, 72100-72104, 72106; structdata/fs_itemlist.lua:137-148).

    Lore#

    • A Journey is a loop of at most four. The game's loop limit is 4 (logic/battle/battledata.lua:45), which is why the achievement "Legendary Courier" speaks of the fourth Journey (Achievement 20005).
    • Sixty story items vanish. When a new Journey starts, FS_ItemList:Restart deletes every item whose GamePlusRemove is 1: 60 items, almost all of the plot objects (Scarlet Meteor Fragments, Sword fragments, Pillar keys, the Seal marker, the rune stones and the Egg of the Bearer).
    • Flags gate the dialog. Every dialog row can carry ShowQuestId, HideQuestId, CompleteQuestId and RevertQuestId; rows such as the Mist Camp menu (StoryDialog 2441-2445) only exist while the lamp flags are set.
    • Few rows carry a DLC flag. DLCShow is set on only 7 of 3,117 rows (5 with value 1, 2 with value 2); the DLC chapter's 412 rows live in their own StoryId 9 instead (ES_StoryDialog).
    • 2 more in the "Hidden mechanics" section of Lore.

    Marcions#

    • The Stones combine into one item. The Past, Present and Future stones are three separate key items (ItemData 10013-10015); a ritual joins them into the Puzzle Ball, which is what actually opens the mist (UIQuest 10505).
    • Marcion items are removed on a new Journey. The three Stones and related keys have GamePlusRemove = 1; the coin and the Argil modeling clay do not (ItemData 10013-10015, 10045, 10050; structdata/fs_itemlist.lua:137-148).
    • Katib's mist is not the Sendrils. One character states that the mist there is different from the Sendrils (StoryDialog story 5 row 1187).

    Seals of Yekub#

    Timelines#

    • Story ids do not follow the visible chapter order. Edith is story 4 and Katib story 5, but you reach Katib first.
    • There is no clock. The only time phrases (a few months, more than a decade) come from dialog and quest text.
    • The campaign order is enforced by quest flags, not by maps. For example Edith's gate to the Blessedland is opened by a key that Elena hides, and this is why the ending of the Edith chapter is the beginning of the Blessedland ...
    • Region blurbs. The poetic intro for Ichthyosauria in the Documents log (30901) was left in Chinese in the English texts. Every other region blurb is translated.
