    Sanity

    Sanity

    Character meter

    Base maximum
    100 for every hero
    Abnormal state
    at or below 70% of maximum
    Lunatic state
    at or below 50% of maximum
    At zero
    the hero dies
    Raised by
    Consumables, kills in the maze, jellyfish jars, altars, talents
    Lowered by
    fighting, special enemy moves, time in the Dark Mist, certain items

    Sanity is the third meter every hero carries besides health and stamina. It starts full, drains while you fight enemies or linger in the Dark Mist, and decides which of three mental states the hero is in: Normal, Abnormal (the UI word for the first stage) and Lunatic. Each hero has a different set of penalties and bonuses in the lower two states, and many trophies and talents only work there.

    The meter#

    Every hero has a base maximum of exactly 100 sanity. The game computes it from the hidden attribute row of the hero (hidden attribute 1, which maps to 1,000 on the attribute curve, times a 100/1000 rate), so there is no hero that starts higher or lower.

    StateRule (M = maximum sanity)At M = 100Notes
    Normalsanity above 0.7 x M (rounded up)71 to 100No penalties.
    Abnormalsanity at or below ceil(0.7 x M)70 down to 51Hero-specific effects begin. See Abnormal state. Nearby ordinary enemies switch to their aggressive move sets (inferred from the AI flag).
    Lunaticsanity at or below ceil(0.5 x M)50 down to 1Sanity can no longer be refilled above 50. See Lunatic state.
    Deadsanity reaches 00Counts as a lethal hit, exactly like losing all health.

    The talent Sanity (every hero tree) adds 5% / 10% / 15% to the maximum. Because both thresholds are fractions of the maximum, they move up with it:

    Sanity talent levelMaximumAbnormal at or belowLunatic at or below
    none1007050
    11057453
    21107755
    31158158

    A larger maximum therefore does not move you farther from the stages in percentage terms; it only gives a bigger pool above them.

    Damage costs sanity (campaign)#

    Whenever the hero lands damage on an enemy in the campaign, the game takes SanDamage x HP removed / enemy max HP sanity from the hero. A "SanDamage" of 5 therefore means: depleting that enemy's whole health bar costs 5 sanity, whether you do it in one blow or many.

    Across the 2,747 enemy placements in the data:

    GroupSanDamage
    Ordinary campaign enemies (about 700 of the 800 non-boss rows in chapters 1 to 9)usually 5; a few 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 15, 20, 25
    Elites and mini-bosses20 to 130 (for example the club-wielding elite, internal 20030, has 65 and 110)
    Chapter bosses70 to 130 (see below)
    All other rows (the rest of chapters 1 to 9, all of chapter 8, and every maze monster)0

    Boss values: Maddened Ascetic 90 (75 in its later chapter 7 appearance), Holy Father 85, Jenny of Exilium 100, Edith 70, Frollo 55, Elmar 20, Elena 55, Fused Sprenger 120, Proxy Higgins 120 (130 in the second set of chapter rows, story 1006), Usurper 130, and the three DLC bosses 130 (The First Messenger and the Terror Obsessions cost nothing). Each chapter's rows exist twice in the table (story ids such as 1 and 1001) with the same values unless noted. A value above 100 sounds fatal, but boss rooms pin your sanity at a floor, see Lunatic state.

    The talent Devout (every hero) cuts this particular loss by 15%. It does not reduce the "enemy move" losses below.

    Enemy moves that hit sanity directly#

    Only four enemy models carry sanity damage on specific attacks:

    EnemyMove (internal id)Sanity per hit
    Maddened Ascetic (20007)special 615
    Holy Father (20014)eleven moves (4-1 to 12-2)2 to 5 each
    Jenny of Exilium (20022)move 4-210
    A deer enemy (20029)move 5-15

    These are scaled down at long range for moves with a damage-distance falloff (full value inside the move's minimum distance, sqrt(1 - x^2) of it in between where x is how far you are between the minimum and maximum distance, and nothing beyond the maximum), and halved by the trophy Loneliness Whisper (its text says "reduced chance", but the code halves the amount; every hit still lands).

    No hero skill in the data carries a sanity-gain value (the code would add a skill's SanAdd on executions and backstabs, but every SkillData row has 0), and the talent types for gaining sanity from kills, events or skills (SanKillAdd, SanEventAdd, SkillSanAdd) exist in the code but are used by no talent.

    When sanity does not change#

    Sanity is not lost at all:

    • while the host hero is dead or the game has stopped attacks (cutscenes);
    • in dream sequences;
    • in Arcade mode outside boss fights (Arcade mode);
    • from hero allies, which ignore sanity damage entirely outside the maze.

    Sanity belongs to the hero#

    Each hero stores its own sanity in the save. Switching heroes carries the old hero's percentage to the new one, but switching is blocked while the current hero is Lunatic. After each hero is created, saved sanity is clamped to that hero's current maximum, and a hero loaded while at or below the Lunatic threshold is re-locked at the cap.

    Hidden mechanics#

    • Campaign kills never refill sanity. The "SanKill" gain is zero for every ordinary enemy in chapters 1 to 9, and the only non-zero rows there are the four chapter bosses (+200) and the two club-elite entries (+200). Killing in the maze is the opposite: it costs nothing and refills 1 to 10.
    • The sanity cost is proportional to damage dealt. Weak enemies you barely scratch cost almost nothing; a huge-health enemy costs the same total as a small one.
    • Large damage-to-sanity numbers on bosses are safe. In a boss fight the minimum is pinned at the Lunatic bound, so a "130" boss cannot kill you through sanity.
    • Sanity potions ignore your defences. The sanity statuses (Elixir of Sanity, Elixir of Hallucination, Vertebra Nail, the ground effects) are tagged with the physical defence type, but their defence scale (Param3) is 0, and the status code multiplies your resistance by that scale before using it. Every tick is therefore simply ceil(base x (1 + item talent bonus)): 1 for the Elixir of Sanity, 5 for Hallucination and the Nail, 2 for the ground effects.
    • "Improved Effect" doubles the Elixir of Sanity. It multiplies the per-second value by 1.1 / 1.2 / 1.3 (talent levels 1 to 3) and the result is rounded up, so the 1 always becomes 2: the elixir gives about 30 instead of 15 over its duration. The same talent raises a Hallucination tick from 5 to 6 / 6 / 7. "Prolonged Duration" lengthens both elixirs by 10% / 20% / 30%. The Vertebra Nail is on neither list.
    • Benita's potion resistance also halves sanity elixirs. While she is Abnormal or Lunatic and carries any of her drug-resistance stacks, every potion-type status tick is halved before rounding up: Hallucination 5 becomes 3, the Elixir of Sanity stays at 1 (even with Improved Effect, 1.1 x 0.5 rounds up to 1).
    • The Lunatic lock is not rounded. Refills stop at exactly 0.5 x maximum (57.5 with three Sanity talent levels), while the Lunatic test rounds its bound up (58), so the lock can never lift you out of the state.
    • Statue and frog numbers are on a doubled scale. The code halves every sanity cost and gain printed on these offers (inferred reason: they were written for a 0 to 200 scale), so a "Dedicate 10 Sanity" statue really takes 5.
    • Several talent descriptions point the wrong way. "Adapt to Abnormality" talents read as additional penalties but are reductions of the penalty. "Lucky Madness" claims to work only in the Abnormal state, but the drop code applies it always.
    • Lost Valley and the maze never charge for damage. Sanity-by-damage is disabled in the Lost Valley, and maze monsters carry a SanDamage of 0.
    • Unused data. The constants for a "sanity first-kill" reduction table (10 / 5 / 1 kills, 100% / 60% / 20%), SanDeadRate 0.6, and the Terrence dodge-close rate 700 are defined but never read.
