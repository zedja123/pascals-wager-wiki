    Attributes

    Game system

    Visible attributes
    6 (Strength, Dexterity, Vigor, Intellect, Vitality, Endurance)
    Hidden attribute
    1 (fixed, cannot be raised)
    Points per level
    1 (levels 2 to 99, so 98 in total)
    Cap per attribute
    99
    Result
    42 hero stats, see Character stats

    Attributes are the six numbers on a hero's Stats page that you raise with the points you earn from Leveling. Every combat stat on that page (Health, Attack, Defence, Stamina, resistances, crit chance and so on, see Character stats) is computed from them. A seventh, hidden attribute supplies the fixed stats that never change with level, such as Sanity, Rage and the stamina regeneration rate. This page gives the exact formula, the curves, and what one point is worth for each hero.

    Overview#

    • Each hero starts with a fixed set of attribute scores (table below).
    • Every level gained at an altar gives 1 attribute point to spend on any attribute. At level 99 a hero has therefore spent or banked 98 points.
    • A single attribute cannot go above 99.
    • Each stat is the sum of "weight x curve" over the attributes. The curve (how much one score is worth) is the same for all six attributes. The weight (how much of that curve a hero gets from an attribute) differs per hero and per stat. This is the only thing that makes Benita a caster and Viola a glass-cannon duelist.

    Names of the attributes#

    The game's scripts name the six slots Strength, Spirit, Brave, Dexterous, Tolerant and Generous. These internal names do not describe what the slots do, and the on-screen labels are stored in the Unity interface rather than in the game's data tables, so they cannot be read from the data. This wiki therefore labels the slots by what they actually drive (inferred):

    SlotScript nameWiki nameMain role

    2SpiritDexterityCrit rate and Crit damage for every hero; Viola's Attack

    The formula#

    For each of the 42 hero stats, the game does the following (FS_HeroData.GetPropertyAllByList):

    stat = floor( sum over attributes a of  weight[hero, stat, a] x curve[a, points, stat]  / 1000 )
    • weight comes from the HeroPropertyData table (Attribute_1 to Attribute_7 columns). It is in per mille: 850 means "85% of the curve".
    • curve comes from the HeroAttributeData table, indexed by attribute number, the score (1 to 99) and the stat (Property_1 to Property_42).
    • Only weights above 0 are used. A stat with no positive weight is 0.
    • The sum is rounded down once, after all six attributes plus the hidden one have been added.

    A reimplementation of this loop reproduces every starting-stat value listed under Starting stats below.

    Worked example, Terrence at the start (Strength 15, Dexterity 9, Vigor 14):

    • Health = 900 x Health curve(14) / 1000 = 900 x 944 / 1000 = 849.6, shown as 849.
    • Attack = (850 x Attack curve(15) + 50 x Attack curve(9)) / 1000 = (850 x 68 + 50 x 43) / 1000 = 59.95, shown as 59.

    What is shown on the Stats page for the hero (and used in battle) is that base value plus Trophies, Talents and difficulty effects. How those are layered on top is described on Character stats.

    The curves#

    All six visible attributes use the same table for every stat that playable heroes use: the curves of attributes 1 to 6 are identical at all 99 points. (The one exception is the four elemental attack stats, properties 19 to 22: only attribute 1 has a curve for them and attributes 2 to 6 are all 0. Only the First Messenger has weight on those stats, and only on attribute 1.) The curves fall into a few families, and several stats share one family:

    Points between the rows follow the table in HeroAttributeData. Key features:

    • Attack, Defence, Health and Stamina rise fastest between about 10 and 30 points, then flatten hard. Attack gains about 5 per point from 20 to 30, 2.5 from 30 to 40, 1.3 from 40 to 50, 0.8 from 50 to 60 and only 0.2 per point beyond 60. Health gains about 34 per point from 20 to 30 and just 1.1 per point beyond 80.
    • Their ceilings are exactly 200 (Attack), 210 (Defence), 1871 (Health), 840 (Stamina) and 2000 (Shields) at 99 points.
    • Crit rate is perfectly linear: +1 per point (0.1 percent), from 50 at 1 point to 148 at 99.
    • Crit damage keeps accelerating until about 50 points (up to +3.9 per point between 40 and 50), then slows. It reaches 400 at 99 points.
    • Because the curve is the same for every attribute, for Attack, Defence, Health, Stamina and Shields a point is worth the most when the attribute is in the 10 to 30 range, whichever attribute it is. This is why spreading points over several attributes beats a single-attribute build for the first few dozen points.

    Stats every hero gets from the "hidden" attribute#

    • Vigor is by far the best Health source for every hero at the start (+319 to +395 Health for 10 points for the five older heroes, +118 for Elena who starts at 34) but gives only +76 to +96 once you are past 40.

    Spending points#

    • Points are added on the Stats page. The page refuses to add a point once the attribute shows 99 (playerinfoctrl.lua 1593, 1615) and only lets you take back points added in the current, unconfirmed session (1653).
    • The Stats page shows the new stat values live (PlayerInfoCtrl.GetPropertyTemp, which calls the same formula) before you confirm.
    • When you confirm, any Health you add also heals you: the hero's current Health rises by the change in maximum Health (AddHeroAttribute, playerinfoctrl.lua 10615-10642).
    • Respec. FS_HeroData:ResetAttribute sets every spent point back to 0 and refunds level - 1 points. The only caller in the game scripts is the story-quest action ResetAttribute (StoryQuest row 1068, story 5). I found no other place that calls it (inferred: there is no free respec in normal play; a "Reset Attributes" string exists in the text table, but the button lives in the Unity interface).

    Enemies#

    Enemies run through the same code with isHost = false, which switches to the EnemyAttributeData table. An enemy's attributes are its HeroData base plus level - 1 on every one of the six visible slots (FS_HeroData.GetPropertyAll). The enemy curves match the hero curves for the first 22 or more points and then diverge (Health from point 23, Attack from 26, Crit rate from 52). Enemy attributes are also capped at 99. At 99 points:

    Stat at 99 pointsHero curveEnemy curve
    Attack200456
    Health18714320
    Defence210200

    Enemy models also use different weights. Across the 67 enemy and boss rows, slot 1 feeds Attack, Crit damage and the four elemental attack stats, slot 2 feeds Crit resistance, slot 3 Health (and stamina recovery while guarding), slot 4 Soul Dew, Sanity, Pierce resistance and the four elemental resistances, slot 5 Pierce resistance, Defence, Rage decay and the four elemental resistances, and slot 6 Stamina and Power. The hidden slot supplies Crit resistance and Health on some rows, a share of Stamina, Poise, Poise regen, Resilience, the stamina delay value and the Human, Beast and Sendril type flags (properties 38 to 40). See Enemies for individual models.

    Hidden mechanics#

    • One curve, six attributes. The game never tells you that a point of Strength and a point of Vitality are worth exactly the same on the underlying curve; only the hero's weights decide. So when you want a raw stat (Health, Attack, Defence), the first points into the attribute with the largest weight are worth far more than the points after about 35; spreading across the attributes a hero has weight in is usually more efficient than stacking one.
    • Steep up to about 30, flat after 60. The stats are built to reward a spread of roughly 20 to 40 points in each attribute you care about. Moving an attribute from 60 to 99 buys less Attack than 6 points from 20 to 26.
    • Pierce resistance is 5 times the Defence curve. Intellect and Vitality each carry 250% weight, so Pierce resistance climbs five times as fast in total as ordinary Defence (260 at the start against Defence 63 for Terrence). Attacks whose defence type is Pierce are resisted by this stat instead of Defence (animalbase_value.lua 1826-1836), so Vitality and Intellect matter most against them.
    • Elemental resistances are always four identical stats. Fire, Poison, Arcane and Dark resistance have the same weights for every hero, so you cannot specialise.
    • Endurance means damage for Jerold and Elena. Their Attack, Crit damage and Crit rate all draw on Endurance; their Strength is a minor source.
    • Poise has no attribute source. Property 13 (Poise) is 0 for every hero at every attribute setting. All Poise comes from Trophies (and +130 on Casual). Terrence's Poise Regen is also 0 while every other hero has 240 (24 percent of max Poise per second, from the hidden attribute): any Poise he gains from other sources does not regenerate by itself (inferred from the regen formula; see Poise and stagger).
    • The hidden attribute decides class identity. Norwood's Rage decay is half of Terrence's (50 against 100 per mille per second), and Jerold's and Elena's is 60. Jerold and Elena also have a Stamina exhaustion value of 92 instead of 100, which adds a 0.8 second lock after the bar hits zero (Stamina). You cannot change any of these: the hidden attribute is not on the Stats page and the save always writes it as 0 added points.
    • Values above 99 would break the stats. The attribute table has exactly 99 rows per attribute although the code declares a 200 maximum. A score of 100 or more finds no row and contributes 0 (inferred from MS_HeroAttributeData.GetValue). The UI stops you at 99, and the preset builds stay under 35, so this never occurs in normal play.
    • Leveling cannot overspend. The 98-point total is a hard limit: attribPoints rises by exactly 1 per level-up (playerinfoctrl.lua 10595) and nothing else adds attribute points in the scripts.
    • A leftover system. The BufferData table (12 rows, internal names for "Blessing" and "Source" buffs) carries numbers of 10, 20 or 40 in its attribute columns for six of its entries, the Life, Attack and Defence "Blessing" (rows 1 to 3) and "Source" (rows 7 to 9) buffs; their Chinese descriptions speak of raising a level by 1 or 2. The only reader is the guard-mode controller, and the function it calls (EatBuffer) does not exist in the scripts, so this looks like cut content (inferred).
