    This page is the bestiary of Pascal's Wager: 28 regular enemy types, 5 elite types and 2 summoned creatures, each with its own article, plus the 15 bosses, which are covered under Bosses. Everything here comes from the game's data tables and Lua code: the exact stats of every placement, the damage and effects of every attack, the drop tables and the hidden rules behind them.

    About the names. Only bosses have English names in the game files. The regular enemies are known in the data only by their Chinese internal names, so the English names used on this wiki are short translations of those internal names (for example 棒子怪 "club monster" is the Club Fiend). Each article keeps the internal name in its infobox. "Elite enemy" is this wiki's label for the five big, named enemies (Tentacle Monster, Butcher, Boar Man, Little Giant, Noble Knight); the data has no elite flag.

    How enemy stats are built#

    Every enemy has the same 42 stat slots as a hero (HP, attack of each damage type, defence and resistances, Poise, Resilience, crit values, stamina and so on). They are computed once when the enemy is created:

    1. Attributes. The model has seven base attribute values in HeroData (Attribute_1 to Attribute_7). Each placement has a level in StoryMonster. The attribute points used are base + (level - 1) for attributes 1-6 and just base for attribute 7 (the hidden one), capped at 99.
    2. Curves. For each stat the game adds up rate x curve over the seven attributes, where rate comes from HeroPropertyData (per model and stat, in per mille) and curve from EnemyAttributeData (a separate enemy table; heroes use a different one). The sum is divided by 1000 and rounded down.
    3. Placement bonus. The row of StoryMonsterProperty for that exact placement (story, monster id, phase) is added on top, stat by stat. This is where an individual enemy gets extra HP, attack, defence, crit rate, pierce resistance or Poise.
    4. Heroes get talents and trophies on top of this; ordinary enemies do not.

    Worked example: the Club Fiend's HP at level 9 (its Ch.5 placement in loop 1). Only the attributes with a non-zero rate count:

    AttributePoints at level 9HP curve valueRate (per mille)Rate x curve
    311832400332800

    The total is 332800, divided by 1000 and rounded down: 332 HP. The placement's StoryMonsterProperty row adds nothing here, so the in-game value is 332.

    Two consequences that the game never tells you:

    • Poise, Resilience and their regeneration do not grow with level. Their curves are flat, so a loop 4 enemy staggers exactly as easily as a loop 1 one. HP, attack and defence grow a lot.
    • Difficulty does not touch enemies. The Casual adjustments in the stat code only apply to the player's hero; see Difficulty.

    Drops#

    When an enemy dies, each id in its DropId list is one roll in the drop table (ItemDropData):

    • The table is keyed by chapter id and roll id, so id 10014 means "bones" in Ch.1 but something else in another chapter would be a different row set. A group is a list of weighted entries, including "nothing" entries (item 0). One entry is picked with chance weight / total weight, and the amount is a random whole number between the entry's two amounts.
    • Each entry has four columns of item, amounts and weight, one per loop. The game switches columns with the loop (MS_StoryItemDrop.SetLoop). That is how Bone Fragment amounts jump from a handful to a few dozen in New Game Plus.
    • Health or Mana potion entries are left out of the roll (and out of the total weight) while your potion stock of that kind is full, so the other entries become more likely.
    • Talents that raise item drops add the entry's ProbAdd1 or ProbAdd2 weight (5 and 10 in the data) to entries of the matching item type before the roll.
    • Some placements have a FirstDropId: the first time you kill an enemy whose first-kill quest (FirstDropCompleteQuestId) is not yet complete, it rolls that list instead of its normal one and the kill completes the quest. Placements that share a quest share the bonus: only the first one killed uses it.
    • SanDropId lists are extra rolls that happen only while your hero is not in a normal sanity state; the result goes straight into your inventory. See Sanity.
    • The random generator is re-seeded for every roll from the last six digits of the clock (reversed) plus the enemy id and roll number (MathHelper.getRandom), so the result depends on the second in which the kill happens.
    • No table, no drop. The roll looks up the current story id. Ch.8 enemies carry no drop lists at all, and the Dark Mist placements in story 101 name drop ids that have no group in that story, so they drop nothing (inferred from the lookup). Maze enemies use a separate table (StoryMazeDrop); see Maze rewards. Obsession Challenge fights drop nothing.

    Example: Ch.1 roll 10014 (Bone Fragment / Big Bone).

    AI and senses#

    Each placement carries its own senses in StoryMonster. A value of -1 means "use the default" (sight radius 10 m, sight angle 55 degrees, hearing radius 8 m, hearing angle 180 degrees).

    FieldMeaningTypical values (chapter placements)
    SightRadiusDistance within which it can notice you in front of it0 to 100, median 13.4
    SightAngleWidth of its vision cone0 to 180, median 54.8
    HearingRadiusDistance within which it can hear you, inside its hearing angle (inferred from the getter names)0 to 100, median 5.7
    LoseRadiusIf you get farther than this it gives up the chase (unless it is mid-attack); it also gives up if you stand on a staircase or it cannot find a path0 to 100, median 7
    CooperationRangeIdle enemies at their post with a range above 0 take over the target of a nearby ally (inferred from GetTargetCooperation)0 to 22.4, median 0
    • Notice, roar, attack. After spotting you, an enemy with an alert skill turns to face you (up to 1.5 s or until it is within 15 degrees), then plays its roar (Threat move) before attacking. It skips the roar if you are already within 5.5 m, farther than 17 m, or if it has just been hit. Those numbers are constants of the Alert behaviour.
    • Idle behaviour. IsRelax selects what it does when calm: 1 = idle animation after a wait, 2 = looping idle, 3-5 = it plays spawn animation 1, 2 or 3 (moves #114 to #116) when you come near. IsAlert = 1 is the normal alert behaviour, and 541 of the 636 chapter enemy placements use alert type 1; 532 use idle type 1.
    • Easy AI. 27 chapter placements (dogs, club, archer and poison fiends, axemen) have AILevel 2 (Easy); the rest use 1 (Normal). In the AI files that check it, Easy enemies stop a combo a hit earlier.
    • Combos and cooldowns. Each enemy has an AI file (attack<model id>.lua) with per-move range windows, cone angles, chance per decision and cooldowns; the Attacks table of each article lists them. Moves with no entry are chained as follow-ups of a combo.
    • Drop attack. Enemies that have a jump chop (move #111) use it when they fall onto you from a ledge. It is unblockable (Guard Break 999).

    Hidden mechanics#

    • Flat attack dominates early, level dominates later. An enemy hit is attack stat x BaseRate/1000 + flat Attack. At level 1 the flat part is most of it (the Club Fiend's first swing is 92 + 133 = 225); in loop 4 the level-scaled stat is most of it (about 957). See Damage formula.
    • Poise and Resilience never scale. See above and Poise and stagger.
    • Dark Mist enemies scale with your hero, not the chapter. In the Dark Mist scenes (stories 101-105, scene ids 20000-29999) every enemy's level is its placement level for the current loop plus your hero's level plus 10, and the StoryMonsterProperty bonuses are skipped. The placement levels themselves are 1-13 in loop 1 and always 1 in loops 2-4, so in New Game Plus a Dark Mist enemy is simply hero level + 11. Levelling up before entering the mist makes its enemies stronger.
    • Some chapters drop nothing. Ch.8 has no drop lists, and the Dark Mist rolls find no table (see Drops).
    • Drops are time-seeded, and amounts jump roughly tenfold between loop 1 and loop 4 for common bone drops.
    • Placement bonuses. 192 of the 636 chapter enemy placements get flat stat bonuses on top of the formula (most often extra Physical attack, crit rate, defence or Pierce resistance).
    • The roar window. Enemies roar (a free opening) when they notice you between 5.5 and 17 m away; get inside 5.5 m first, or hit them first, to skip it.
    • Unused enemy. The Wheel Monster (20004) is fully set up but never placed.
    • Unbreakable grabs. Set-piece moves such as the Little Giant's bear hug and self-destruct and the Noble Knight's grab carry armour 999 or 9999 (an extra Poise pool of that many points while the move's armour window is active), so they are effectively unbreakable while they play; a few regular enemies have one such move too (see each Attacks table).
