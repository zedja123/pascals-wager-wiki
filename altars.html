    Altars

    Altars

    Game system

    In-game name
    Altar
    Interact range
    3 world units, facing the altar
    Icon visible from
    7 world units
    Autosave
    Every 60 s while exploring
    Rest effect
    Full heal, Health Potions, enemies respawn
    Leveling
    See Leveling

    An altar is the game's checkpoint and workshop. Standing at one lets you rest, spend bones to raise your level (Leveling), spend attribute points (Attributes), add talents (Talents) and craft with the Alchemy menu (Alchemy). It also saves the game and sets where you wake up after dying.

    Using an altar#

    • An altar icon appears when you are within 7 world units. The "Altar" action button appears when you are within 3 units and your character is facing toward the altar. It only works from the normal movement state, not in the middle of an attack or other action.
    • The first time you touch a given altar it is lit. The lit state is stored per area and per altar number in your save. Lighting has no gameplay effect beyond the animation and effects; the Dedication and crafting animations only play on a lit altar.
    • Opening the altar menu runs the save routine at once (see below). Closing it saves again.
    • In Arcade mode, an extra altar (number 99) appears after a stage is cleared and lets you move on to the next stage. In Arcade the Dedication, crafting, bag, trophy and task tabs are hidden; the rest text says to rest for health, Health Potions and new talents.

    The pause-menu version of the same screen greys out the whole Altar tab. Blessing is not an altar service: it is handled in the exchange screen (the "Benita" transaction type, see Merchants and Blessing). The altar UI contains no travel option either; travel is by Carriage or the whistles (see Leveling).

    Recovery (resting)#

    Choosing Recovery fades the screen, then does the following in order.

    1. Runs the same respawn routine your hero gets after dying: the hero is put back at the altar's spawn marker, Sanity is set to full and the Sanity lock states are cleared, and every property (Health included) is refilled to its maximum. Negative statuses are removed; buffs stay. Rage and the trophy and skill shields are only clamped to their maximum when you rest alive, but are set to 0 when you respawn after dying.
    2. Wipes the death list: every enemy that you killed since your last rest is created again. Bosses and other enemies whose appearance depends on a story quest do not come back once that quest is complete (inferred from the quest show/hide checks in the spawn code). In Arcade mode the death list is not wiped: only the monsters on a short fixed refresh list are removed from it.
    3. Restores every hero's Health Potions to their maximum (see Leveling for the counts).
    4. Deletes loot that is lying on the ground (drops from killed enemies you did not pick up), except your map markers and any Heggie pile.
    5. Removes summoned Sanity monsters and called-in helper heroes.
    6. Refills shop stock that is flagged to refresh, for every merchant (see Merchants).
    7. Resets the crow companion and the frog satisfaction list.
    8. Closes the altar menu, which saves the game (the position is stored as the respawn point again).
    9. Plays the rest effect, then removes and re-applies the trophy-granted protections No Death, Shield and Small Shield if you wear trophies with those effects (see Trophies).

    Resting is exactly the same routine as respawning after death, which is why dying also resets enemies and refills potions. Rest is different in what you keep: dying costs Bone Fragments, resting costs nothing.

    Respawn points#

    The respawn point is the position of the last altar whose menu you opened. Several other events also move it:

    • Opening the altar menu (and closing it) sets both the respawn and the "born" position to the altar's spawn marker.
    • Arriving at a new area by Carriage sets the respawn point where you arrive.
    • Changing scene (when it is not the first load) stores your current spot as the respawn point.
    • The 60-second autosave only writes your position for loading the game; it does not change the respawn point.

    After death the game moves you to the area of your respawn point if it differs from the current one, and places you at the stored position.

    Hidden mechanics#

    • Altars advance the story. Some altars complete quests when you open them. The table StorySave lists 24 altar entries (story 1: altars 3, 4, 5; story 2: 2, 3, 4; story 3: 1, 2, 3; story 4: 1 to 4; story 7: 1 to 5; story 9, the Stonefin Shoal / DLC region (inferred): 1 to 5; plus one entry for story 99 whose quest fields are all 0, so it does nothing). Each triggers a quest only when its "show" quest is done and its "hide" quest is not, so the effect happens once, the first time the condition is met. Visiting altars in a new place can therefore trigger scenes, doors or new dialog.
    • Opening is saving. The game saves when the altar menu opens, not just when you rest. Closing it without resting still counts as a rest-free visit.
    • Dropped loot vanishes. Because Recovery clears the ground, pick up drops before resting. The same clearing happens when you die. Ordinary ground loot does survive saving and quitting; only a rest or a death removes it.
    • Loading refills potions. When a story scene is loaded (not the very first load, and not inside the maze), every hero's Health Potions are set to maximum again, so reloading a save is another free potion refill.
    • Merchant stock. Limited stock on items flagged "refresh" returns to maximum whenever you rest or die, not on a timer.
    • Level and Dedication. The experience bar and all unlocked bones show in the Dedication tab; at level 99 the tab refuses further spending. Details in Leveling.
    • Trophy protections. Resting clears and re-applies the No Death, Shield and Small Shield trophy statuses, so those effects are renewed by a rest (whether they also renew on other occasions is not covered here).
    • Facing rule. The altar test combines squared distance with a dot product on your facing direction, so you can be standing right next to an altar and still get no button if you are facing away from it.

    Trivia#

    • The altar's open state is a two-state animation (closed, open). A third "empty" state exists in the code but is never set.
    • Other altar-related animation triggers named in the code are "dedication" and "alchemy". They play when you spend bones or craft something.
