    Damage formula

    Every damaging move in Pascal's Wager has an attack type and a defence type. The attack type picks which of the attacker's attack stats is used. The defence type picks which of the target's resistance stats reduces the hit. There are six of each, plus an "Other" value (0) that means none. This page lists them and explains how much a resistance actually protects you; the full calculation is on Damage formula.

    The six types#

    IdDamage typeAttacker's stat (property id, UI name)Target's resistance (property id, UI name)
    1PhysicalPhysicDamage (1, "Attack")PhysicResist (10, "Defence")
    2FireFireDamage (19, "Element Damage")FireResist (23, "Element Res")
    3PoisonPoisonDamage (20, "Posion Damage", sic)PoisonResist (24, "Poison Res")
    4ArcaneArcaneDamage (21, "Arcane Damage")ArcaneResist (25, "Arcane Res")
    5DarkDarkDamage (22, "Shadow Damage")DarkResist (26, "Shadow Res")
    6PiercePierceDamage (31, untranslated)PierceResist (32, untranslated)
    0Othernonenone: defence is ignored

    The attack type and defence type are separate columns (AttackType, DefenceType) on every move. Almost always they match. The exception is moves with a Physical attack and an "Other" defence type, which ignore the target's defence completely: 201 enemy move rows and 36 move rows of the playable heroes (mostly guard, dodge and similar actions).

    The playable heroes' attack stats for Fire, Poison, Arcane, Dark and Pierce are all 0: no attribute raises them (HeroPropertyData has no weights for those properties on heroes 10001-10005 and 10007; only The First Messenger, 10006, gets Fire/Poison/Arcane/Dark attack, all from the same first attribute that drives its Physical attack). A thrown Fire Jar or Throwing Knife therefore deals exactly its flat power (Param1: 200 for the Fire Jar, 180 for the Throwing Knife, 250 for the Huge Fang and Poison Awl, 40 for the status-throwables). The defence step then reduces it.

    How resistance reduces damage#

    Resistance is not a percentage. The game compares the attack value with the resistance value:

    attack ÷ resistance≤ 12351020
    Damage taken (% of attack)30%36.7%43.3%56.7%90%95%

    (Formula: 30% if the ratio is 1 or less, (2r + 7) / 30 between 1 and 10, and 1 − 1/r above 10; a resistance of 0 lets the full attack through.)

    What this means in practice:

    • Against enemies, resistance matters most when your attack value is close to it. Once your attack is ten times their resistance, they barely resist at all.
    • Your own resistance protects you most against weak hits. Against a huge hit (more than 10× your resistance) it barely helps, and it can never reduce a hit below 30%.
    • Traps use a different formula: 1 − res / (res + 100), so 100 resistance halves trap damage regardless of the trap's power.

    Ways to change resistances#

    • Trophy properties: Defence (10), Element Res (23, which only protects against Fire), Poison, Arcane and Shadow Res (24-26). See Random trophy properties.
    • Conditional trophies (only while you are the one being hit):
    TriggerPhysical defenceElemental resistances (Fire/Poison/Arcane/Dark)
    Health fullStrong Spirit Casket (+450%)Seal of the Pious (+450%)
    Health ≤ 50%Brass Ring of Handel (+7.5%), The Rose (+15%)logic type 126 (used only by a cut test trophy)
    Abnormal sanityPoison Arrowleaf, Pagan Icon, Executioner's Gloves (+12.5%)Mist Bracer, Stag Rings (+12.5%)
    • Lunatic states (see Lunatic state): Norwood loses 50% of his Fire/Poison/Arcane/Dark resistance; Jerold loses 50% of his Dark resistance. Traps halve Norwood's lowered elemental resistance a second time (25% of normal; see Damage formula).
    • Marcion Puzzle Ball makes you completely immune to Pierce-type hits (the hit is cancelled before any calculation) and to Pierce-type status effects. No enemy move in the skill table is Pierce-type, so in practice it protects against Pierce-type statuses such as the Marcion poison mist its description mentions (inferred).

    Hidden mechanics#

    • "Element Res" is really Fire resistance. The stat the menus call "Element Res" (and "Element Damage" for attack), named "Elemental resistance" (元素抗性) on trophy properties, is property 23. The damage code reads property 23 only for Fire damage, and Poison, Arcane and Dark each have their own property (24-26). The conditional trophies above, on the other hand, really do cover all four elements.
    • Attack type and defence type are independent. A move can use one stat for attack and another resistance for defence. In the shipped data they only differ for "Physical attack / no defence" moves, which ignore the target's defence. 201 enemy move rows do this.
    • Pierce is the enemies' hidden armour. Enemy Pierce resistance is many times their other defences (up to 20×). A Throwing Knife's 180 flat attack is already below a level-1 enemy's 240 Pierce resistance, so it deals 54 damage (the 30% floor) to almost everything, on every cycle.
    • No trophy can give you an elemental or Pierce attack. Random trophy properties only roll Attack, crit, HP, defence, the four resistances and the race bonuses (types 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 11, 15, 23-26, 35-37), never properties 19-22 or 31. Thrown weapons therefore always hit for their flat power, and race bonuses never apply to them.
    • Resistance is read from the current maximum, rounded up. The defence value includes buffs, debuffs and lunatic-state changes, is rounded up and can't go below 0. When you are hit, the conditional trophies then multiply it.
    • Status damage ignores resistances. Bleed, Poison, Burn and other damage-over-time effects deal fixed damage and never go through this calculation. See Status effects.
