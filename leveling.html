    Leveling

    Game system

    In-game name
    Altar Level
    Level cap

    Experience source

    Points per level

    Leveling in Pascal's Wager does not use experience from kills. Enemies and the world drop bone items, and you spend them in the Dedication tab of an altar to raise your Altar Level. Every level up grants one attribute point (see Attributes) and nothing else. The level is the same for every hero, and it caps at 99.

    Overview#

    • Kills never give experience directly. The experience bar only moves when you "dedicate" bones at an altar.
    • Four bone items carry experience: Bone Fragment, Big Bone, Fractured Body and Filthy Core.
    • The game calls the level "Altar Level" (see the level cap message and the New Journey confirmation text). Save slots record the level of the default hero (Terrence).
    • Talents are not bought with levels. They use Immundus stones (see Talents and Immundus).

    Three other items (Anthozoan Bones, Dominator Crystal, Spectre Mushroom) sit in the same code list but carry 0 experience and are not shown in the Dedication tab, which lists only the four above. Spectre Mushroom is the Dark Mist (maze) coin type. See Currencies.

    The Dedication tab shows a bone only after its quest flag is set. That a bigger bone also unlocks the smaller ones is inferred, not confirmed.

    Bone Fragments double as the main shop currency, so every purchase you make is also experience you did not gain. See Merchants.

    Formula (derived from the table): the cost of leaving level L is 125 at L=1, 275 at L=2, and 200 x L - 150 for every level from 3 to 98. The running total to reach level L is 100 x L^2 - 250 x L + 250 for L of 3 or more. Reaching level 99 costs 955,600 experience, which is about 38,200 Bone Fragments, 4,780 Big Bones, 765 Fractured Bodies or 128 Filthy Cores.

    Spending bones (Dedication)#

    1. Open an altar (see Altars) and pick the Dedication tab. Only altars allow it; the pause menu greys the tab out.
    2. Choose a bone type and press confirm. One press spends one item.
    3. Excess experience carries over: if one Filthy Core crosses several levels you gain all of them, and the leftover stays on the bar.
    4. Each level crossed adds one attribute point to each hero and shows the attribute-point notice.

    Holding the confirm button repeats the action. After half a second of holding it spends one item every few frames, speeds up after about one second and again after two seconds, and after three seconds each tick spends six items (two Filthy Cores per tick). The thresholds are tied to your frame rate setting (144 when it is set to unlimited). Near the cap the game never spends more items than needed to reach level 99.

    How experience is gained#

    SourceDetails
    Enemy dropsEach enemy points to a drop group for its area. A group lists several weighted rows, and one row is rolled (a row's chance is its Prob over the total of the group). The four numbered columns in each row are the four New Game Plus loops (loop 4 and above use the last one). In loop 1, 26 groups can give Bone Fragments (1 to 50 per drop) and 19 can give Big Bones (up to 8); in loop 4, 24 groups give up to 80 Bone Fragments and 23 give Big Bones.
    World pickupsOne-off items placed in the map: in loop 1 there are 11 Bone Fragment piles totalling 1,076, 58 Big Bone pickups totalling 126, 16 Fractured Body pickups totalling 41 and 5 Filthy Core pickups totalling 5.
    SellingSpare bones, trophies and other items sell for Bone Fragments at merchants. A Big Bone sells for 7 fragments.
    DoublingThe Potion of Wealth (Consumables) doubles every bone pickup for 10 minutes (600 s).
    TalentsThe drop code lets talents of the "drop rate" kind add 5 or 10 weight points (ProbAdd1 / ProbAdd2) to rows of a chosen item type. The Lucky Madness talents in the talent report name trophies, materials and elixirs, not bones (inferred). See Talents.

    The fixed world pickups of loop 1 add up to about 140,850 experience (1,076 x 25 + 126 x 200 + 41 x 1,250 + 5 x 7,500), roughly a seventh of the 955,600 needed for level 99. The rest has to come from enemy drops, shop trades and later loops, which have larger pickup totals (loop 4: 165 Big Bones, 103 Fractured Bodies, 8 Filthy Cores). Enemies come back whenever you rest at an altar.

    The drop groups that name Fractured Bodies (3 groups: 40099, 50099, 60099) and Filthy Cores (2 groups: 70099, 90099) have a quantity of 0 in every loop and a weight of 100, and the drop code discards rolls of 0, so they give nothing. No other table points at these groups. Those two bones come from world pickups.

    Death and lost bones#

    When your whole party is defeated (see below) the game takes away a share of your Bone Fragments only. Big Bones, Fractured Bodies and Filthy Cores are never lost.

    • The share is a random fraction between 25% and 35% of the Bone Fragments you hold.
    • The result is rounded down. If a fraction was left over, there is a 30% chance of losing one extra.
    • Expected loss is therefore about 30% of your stack.
    • The death screen shows the number lost.

    Unless you wear the World-Devouring Promise trophy, the lost Bone Fragments are gone. With that trophy equipped, a Heggie-guarded pile holding exactly what you lost is created at your respawn position (the trophy text says "where you stand", but the code creates it after you have been moved to the respawn spot, inferred). Walking into it returns the whole amount (pickups of the pile are not doubled by the Potion of Wealth).

    Heggie piles are session-only: the save routine skips them, so quitting the game removes the pile. A second death before you retrieve a pile deletes the old one and creates a new one, if you had any fragments left to lose.

    Other ways to lose Bone Fragments#

    Blowing the Broken Bronze Whistle (return to the nearest Carriage or to the Mist Camp in the maze) runs the same loss routine, but no pile is created, so those fragments are simply spent. The prompt says "some" Bone Fragments; the actual amount is again 25 to 35%, so the whistle is expensive late in the game. The loss is skipped in Arcade mode. The Bone Whistle works differently: it is used up (one whistle per trip, shop price field 50) and takes no fragments.

    Hidden mechanics#

    • One shared level. Spending bones levels up every hero in the save, including heroes you have not unlocked yet. The code adds the same experience to every entry in the hero list. Heroes cannot be levelled separately, so choosing a different team never changes your level. Attribute points are tracked per hero, but each hero earns the same number.
    • Experience resets on each level. The bar always runs from 0 to the next row of HeroExp. Experience is never cumulative inside the save data.
    • Attribute respec. Resetting attributes (see Attributes) sets every hero's free points to level minus 1 and clears all spent points. It runs through a dialog option that consumes one Starry Honey Wine. Merchants in several areas sell the wine for Big Bones, and the price rises with each New Game Plus loop (for example 40, 60, 80, 100 Big Bones in one area).
    • Per-attribute cap. Attribute values cannot exceed 99 even if you have spare points.
    • Dead rows. The experience table has 500 rows, but the game refuses levels above 99. Row 100 holds 0 experience (row 101 holds 19,850, continuing the line), which looks like a data slip; it is unreachable because the game stops at level 99.
    • Health Potion refill. Dying or resting also refills the Health Potion charges of every hero to their maximum (Terrence 3, the other playable heroes 2, plus any talent bonus). See Altars.
    • Both heroes must fall. The penalty only applies when no other hero in your party is alive. If your second hero survives, the game swaps to them and takes nothing.
    • New Game Plus keeps levels. The restart routine removes only items flagged "GamePlusRemove" and resets story progress. Hero levels, experience, attribute points and bones are kept, and the confirm text lists "Achieved Altar Level" among what you keep. See New Game Plus.
    • Challenge and Arcade presets. Starting a challenge hero (challenge menu) or an Arcade hero calls a routine that sets every hero's level to the preset HeroLevel of that challenge or Arcade hero, gives the chosen hero the preset attribute list and (unless told not to) maxes that hero's talents. Whether this preset replaces your main save or lives in a separate run is not shown in the code I read (unverified). See Obsession Challenge and Arcade mode.
    • Level-up tutorial. The first time you reach level 2 a special tutorial pops up.

    Trivia#

    • The code contains a branch for a demo build that hands out stacks of bones (20 Big Bones, 10 Fractured Bodies and 5 Filthy Cores, plus an item the code names ItemID_ExpAdd) when a story loads. It is not active in the Steam version.
    • Experience items cannot be "used" from the bag. Their only use is the Dedication tab.
