    NPCs in Pascal's Wager are the people (and a few creatures) the Courier can talk to between fights: survivors in the villages, helpers who open gates, merchants, companions waiting to join and a few mysterious figures. They have no names in the data tables (the model name is an internal Chinese label), so most are known by their lines. The pages linked below give each character's story, what they hand out and the hidden flags that drive their dialog.

    Black Spirits (merchants)#

    The black sprite model (hero 20003, 31 story-monster rows: 14 in chapters 1 to 7, 5 in the DLC and 12 in the safe-house, maze and boss-rematch stories) is the merchant. They all follow the same script: a greeting, a menu with Trade and Leave, then the shop panel (table TransactionData; see Merchants for stock and prices). Chapter 1 has two merchants, chapter 2 one, chapters 3, 4, 6 and 7 two each, chapter 5 three and the DLC five.

    The farewell line depends on what you did. After the shop closes the game picks the next line with an index: 1 if you bought or sold anything, 2 if you left without trading. Merchants therefore answer with two different lines per shop, for example "Ha-ha, simple human" if you traded and "Ha-ha, cunning human" if you did not, or the grumpy "I've met meager Couriers just like you, and I'm sorry to say they're all dead now" if you left empty-handed in chapter 3. From exchangectrl.lua:23-26, 56 and dialogctrl.lua:991-1023.

    Other single NPCs#

    • The dying woman of the Colossal Grave (hero 20046, chapter 8, monster 8023). The data labels her model "Viola's mother", which suggests who she is (inferred; the dialog does not name her). She asks whether you are still hanging on and says she and her companions sacrificed an innocent Marcion girl and drank the water of a taboo, which turned them into what they are. She no longer remembers whom she is waiting for and gives the Courier a Bracelet (item 10052, quest 85022) engraved with Samarosian words (the item text says it was left by the Mysterious Woman in the Colossal Grave), asking him to find the person who recognizes it and tell them she waited here. The Bracelet is a key item (see Items).
    • Proxy Higgins's body in chapter 6 is not a person you can talk to but a warning prompt; see Blessedland NPCs.

    How the dialog system works#

    Dialog lines live in table StoryDialog. Every row has a type, and the type decides how it is shown (ms_storydialog.lua:7-18):

    TypeName in codeCountUse
    1ClickNext1,686An ordinary line, advanced by a click.
    2IncludeOption198A line that opens a menu of options.
    3Option526One menu entry the player picks.
    4HostWhisper374The active hero's thought, drawn above the player.
    5NpcWhisper117A short line spoken by a nearby NPC (matched by monster id).
    6Transaction41Opens the shop panel of a merchant.
    7CrowWhisper130A line spoken by the crow.
    8Frog40Opens the Heggies panel.
    9, 10Wear, Statue1 eachA "wear" prompt (inferred to be an outfit prompt), and the maze statue prompt (see Maze statues).

    Hidden mechanics#

    • Flags decide everything. Each NPC spot has a dialog entry, a show flag and a hide flag. A spot appears when its show quest is complete and its hide quest is not. Dialog rows have their own show and hide flags, and a spot lists several entry ids; the first entry that passes the flags is shown (inferred from the data layout). This is why NPCs seem to "move" from a hiding place to a cell to a safe spot as you progress.
    • Gold text (a color tag, DBBA7F) marks options that mostly start, hand in or advance a quest (an observed pattern, not stated in the code).
    • Read flags. Each topic option sets a "read" quest id (the 900xx range), which the game uses to remember which topics you have asked about.
    • Quest actions. A dialog line completes quests; a quest can give items (action 2), take items (18), show a quest-log message (19), play a movie (7), play a timeline (25), destroy a monster (5), go to a stage (24) or record an achievement (30). The enum is in controllerstorymode.lua:94-.
    • Random lines. When a row lists several possible next lines (MultiNextId), the game picks one at random among those whose flags pass.
    • Arcade copies. Story ids 1001 to 1007 are Arcade-mode versions of chapters 1 to 7 (the code subtracts 1000 to find the music, controllerstorymode.lua:887-888), and a subset of NPCs is copied there: the little girl and her mother, Patrick's second spot, Eleanor, the Higgins gate, Frollo's last spot, Glenn, the Gravedigger and a few hero spots. See Arcade mode.
    • The crow. Type 7 lines are spoken by the crow that follows the player.
