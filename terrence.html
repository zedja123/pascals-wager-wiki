    Terrence is the first and default playable hero of Pascal's Wager. He fights with a cane for quick strikes and a Knight's Sword for heavy ones, and everything else he does (Lunge, Discipline, Sanction, Rising Slash) is a "combat art" paid for with Rage. This page collects his numbers from the game's own tables: starting stats, how the attributes grow them, every move with its damage, costs, poise and stagger values, and the changes his lunatic state brings.

    Overview#

    The game describes Terrence as a former church executioner who volunteered to become a courier so he could search for his wife, and who has spent years travelling through the Dark Mist. Mechanically he is the baseline hero: the highest starting Defence of the five campaign heroes (63, against 39 to 59), a guard stance with a counter window, three combat moves that start unlocked (Lunge, Parry guard stance, Execution) and three Health Potion slots instead of two.

    His kit has three layers:

    1. Light and Heavy chains that build Rage (3 per Light hit, 6 per Heavy hit).
    2. Combat arts that spend 30 or 100 Rage and are entered by switching from one chain to the other (Light into Heavy, Heavy into Light) or by holding a button. See Rage.
    3. Reaction moves: guard and Counter Attack, Backstab, Execution and the Drop attack.

    Weapons and style#

    Terrence holds two weapons. Light attacks and the Lunge use his cane; Heavy attacks and the sword arts use the Knight's Sword (the game's code names them WeaponStick and WeaponSword). The in-game button prompts describe Light as a swift cane attack, Heavy as a powerful sword attack (hold for a rising cut), Special as guard, and the fourth button as dodge and dash.

    His Special button raises a sword guard with a counter window; see Guarding.

    Moveset#

    Button names: Light, Heavy, Special (guard), Dodge, Interact. "Tap" means press and release at once.

    How to read this table:

    • Rage needed / spent: the Rage you must have to start the move and the amount taken when it triggers. See Rage.

    Lunatic state#

    At low Sanity every hero enters an "abnormal" state with its own side effects (see Sanity and Lunatic state). Terrence's, as the game words it: he cannot dodge twice in a row, his maximum Health falls by 30% and his damage rises by 10%.

    EffectValueWhere it comes from
    Maximum Health-30% of base Health (e.g. 849 becomes about 594)SanCrazyHpSubRate = 300 per mille
    Physical Attack+10% of base Attack (e.g. 59 becomes about 65)SanCrazyPhysicDamageAddRate = 100 per mille
    Second dodgeNot allowed (move 102 is flagged "normal sanity only")SkillSan = 1 on move 102
    Everything elseUnchanged

    Talents on the Sanity page soften or extend this:

    • Adapt to Abnormality: Life (407) shrinks the Health penalty: the reduction is multiplied by (1 - value/1000), giving -20% at rank 1 and -15% at rank 2.
    • Adapt to Abnormality: Dodge (408) lifts the double-dodge ban.
    • Knight's Instinct (409) raises the damage bonus by half: 10% becomes 15%.
    • Lucky Madness (405) adds 5% / 10% to the material drop rate of trophies while abnormal.

    The state begins when Sanity falls to 70% of its maximum (a second, deeper "Crazy" band starts at 50%). Terrence's changes are applied in both bands, because the game only checks "not normal". The 10% bonus is a percentage of the base Attack stat, so it only enlarges the stat part of a hit, not the flat power of the move.

    Hidden mechanics#

    • Three free Health Potion slots. Terrence starts with a capacity of 3 Health Potions, the other heroes with 2; every hero has MpAddNum 4, a separate second potion pool whose exact in-game item I did not trace (unverified). Talents add to the cap (Health Potions: Stock +1 / +2 / +3).
    • Flat power rules the early game. At the starting Attack of 59, a Light hit's raw attack is 61 + 59 x 0.8 = 108: more than half is the flat part. Light and Heavy chains deliver almost the same raw attack per stamina (about 1.7), so the choice between them is about Rage, poise and armour, not damage. At the theoretical maximum of 180 Attack the rates take over and the multipliers matter more.
    • Rage per stamina favours Heavy. A Light hit gives 3 Rage for 65 stamina (0.046 per stamina); a Heavy hit gives 6 Rage for 95 (0.063), about 37% better. A full Heavy chain is 18 Rage, a full Light chain 9.
    • The Lunge is the most stamina-efficient art. At 59 Attack it deals about 2.65 raw attack per stamina (252 for 95), against 2.35 for Discipline, 1.98 for the full Rising Slash and 1.57 for the short one. At 180 Attack the Lunge (4.18) is level with Multi Lunge (4.20) and ahead of everything else; Sanction reaches 4.05.
    • Armour mostly grows with Rage cost. Light attacks have no armour at all, the Heavy chain 26, the 30-Rage arts Lunge and Discipline 64 and the 100-Rage arts (Multi Lunge, Sanction) 94. Rising Slash is the exception: it costs 30 Rage but has only 26. Enemy hits are mostly 51 Poise (see Poise and stagger), so only the arts survive one hit; the 100-Rage arts also carry Guard Break 500, which breaks even strong guards.
    • Rear hits. Every move row has a rear multiplier (BackGuardBreak). Most moves deal x1.25 when they land on an enemy's back and Backstab and the Jump chop deal x2; thrown items have x1. Enemy rows all have a BackDamageRate of 1, so the move's number is the whole bonus. See Backstab.
    • Counter finisher is tied to its target. The second part of the Counter Attack (power 181) can only hit enemies that were just struck by the first part (power 136); other enemies in the way are ignored by it (Hero10001:IsMutekiForSkill). Both parts ignore Poise and cannot crit. See Parry and counter.
    • Moves that cannot crit or be out-poised. Counter Attack, Backstab, Execution and the Jump chop have CritIgnore 1 and ToughnessIgnore 1: they never crit, and they stagger the target whatever its Poise. The opening hit of Sanction also cannot crit. All other hits can crit (see Critical hits).
    • Dodge chains fall back. If Spin Attack is not unlocked, Dodge then Heavy simply starts the ordinary Heavy combo. If it is unlocked, Spin Attack (priority 10) wins.
    • No second Rage payment after Rising Slash. The data row that opens the Heavy combo after Rising Slash carries a 30-Rage requirement, but the chain table sends Rising Slash (and its short and Enhanced versions) straight into the second row of that combo, which has no requirement. Nothing in the chain table points at the Rage-gated first row, so it appears to be unused (inferred from SkillChainData and SkillData rows 8-11).
    • "Not enough Rage" warning only for three moves. Only Lunge, Discipline and Lunge: Knockback have the Tips flag that shows the Rage-insufficient message; the other arts just fail silently.
    • Poise never regenerates for him. Terrence's base Poise is 0 and his Poise Regen is 0. On Casual difficulty he receives +130 Poise that also never regenerates (see Poise and stagger and Difficulty).
    • Stamina recovery. Regeneration is 400 per mille of the bar per second (121 per second at 303 Stamina), but only 100 per mille while guarding. When the bar hits 0 there is no extra lock-out (his recovery-time stat is 100); see Stamina.
    • Breakables. Every attack row also has a DurabilitySub (40 to 999), which the object code (shell.lua) subtracts from destructible objects' durability. The short and full Rising Slash have 999 (inferred: enough to break any breakable in one hit); Rising Slash: Enhanced has 80.
    • Start unlocks. Lunge, Parry and Execution are the only combat moves that begin unlocked. Everything else, including Backstab and Counter Attack, costs talent nodes.

    Trivia#

    • The Obsession Challenge achievement "For Teresa" asks you to beat The First Messenger with any hero except Terrence. See Obsession Challenge and Teresa.
    • Terrence has two outfits: the default and the "Herald Outfit", sold as downloadable content and changed by talking to Glenn's Owl in the carriage. See Skins.
    • The game contains an AI script for Terrence (attack10001.lua) with Counter Attack range and probability tables, and a death hook that notifies the First Messenger boss when a summoned Terrence copy dies. These copies are presumably how the First Messenger fight turns your own hero into an enemy (inferred).
    • In the Chinese internal names the combat arts are "swordsman battle skill light" and "heavy" (full-Rage versions are marked "full"), and the Light chain is literally "light light light".
